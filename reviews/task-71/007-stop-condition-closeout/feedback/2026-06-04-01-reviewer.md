## (1) Suite-runner `parallel_workers_delta` is misleading

**This is the load-bearing one.** Anyone reading the suite
manifest for any future task will see `parallel_workers_delta:
0` and conclude workers didn't spawn — the exact wrong-direction
trail that cost hours during Task 71 (seq 13 BLOCK on packet
003 was filed on this misleading reading; the stale-dylib
triage in seq 15 happened to surface the truth, but only after
significant misdirection).

### Symptom

In `crates/ecaz-cli/src/commands/bench/suite.rs`, the runner
brackets each opted-in load step with
`pg_stat_get_db_parallel_workers_launched(oid)` and writes
`parallel_workers_before`, `_after`, `_delta` into each
`StepRecord` (`results.jsonl` / `suite-manifest.json`). On the
full Task 71 post-install matrix, every one of the 16 load
cells records:

```json
"parallel_workers_before": 0
"parallel_workers_after":  0
"parallel_workers_delta":  0
```

Meanwhile the loader's own
`[loader] ec_ivf build timing: requested_workers=8
workers_launched=7 …` line is correct, and the heap-ingest
speedup is real (877 ms → 274 ms at 100k w=1 → w=8).

### Likely root cause

The bracket capture (`query_parallel_workers_launched` →
`ecaz dev sql` child process) runs in a **separate connection**
from the load step's own connection. PG's
`pg_stat_get_db_parallel_workers_launched(oid)` is database-
cumulative but the stats subsystem requires
`pg_stat_clear_snapshot()` (or a non-`STABLE` read) to refresh
inside a transaction, AND the function reads a snapshot taken
at start of transaction. Two separate short-lived connections
both reading a database-cumulative counter that's actually
incremented by a **third** connection (the load step's own
connection) need careful semantics — and what we observe is
that the increment is invisible to the capture connection.

This is plausibly the standard PG behavior: workers launched
inside a load-step transaction are accounted to that database
via pg_stat, but the **stats reporter** may not flush them
until the load-step backend exits, and the capture happens
**after** the load step starts but **before** it exits. The
capture sees a stale snapshot.

### Fix shape

Two acceptable options. Prefer (a) per FR-038
("extend the runner, not glue around it"):

**(a) Parse the loader timing line into `StepRecord`.** The
parser exists already from f19382afa / bad1027cf:
`parse_ec_ivf_build_timing_line` extracts `workers_launched`,
`requested_workers`, `heap_ingest_us`, etc. Plumb its
`workers_launched` (and optionally `requested_workers`) into
the `StepRecord.parallel_workers_*` fields, replacing the
broken bracket capture. Keep the `capture_parallel_workers:
bool` LoadStep field as the opt-in switch.

**(b) Remove the bracket capture entirely.** Drop the
`capture_parallel_workers` field, drop the
`parallel_workers_before/after/delta` columns, drop the
`query_parallel_workers_launched` helper. Document that the
loader-timing line is the authoritative source.

(a) preserves more useful metadata (per-step delta vs. raw
launched count) and matches the existing FR-038 pattern. Go
with (a) unless there's a reason not to.

### Validation

After the fix, re-run a one-cell probe via
`ecaz dev test ivf-parallel-build-probe` (or just spawn a
single suite step manually) and confirm the new
`parallel_workers_*` fields in `results.jsonl` match the
loader timing line's `workers_launched`. No need to re-run the
full 16-cell matrix.
