# Review: Task 70 / Packet 005 — Retained Frontier Heap — REVERT or REFRAME

Reviewed the source diff at `9bbef9ecf718bab30ef543b5f21d4728267136d0`
(`src/am/ec_diskann/scan.rs`), `ScanCandidate`'s `Ord` impl
(`scan.rs:117-130`), and the packet's phase-split summary.

The coder's own framing — *"this slice is semantics-preserving but not a
clear performance win… please review whether the simpler bounded-retained
structure is worth keeping, or whether this slice should be
shelved/reverted"* — is the right read. I'm recommending revert (or
reframe), not accept-as-perf-win.

## What this packet does

Replaces the sorted `Vec<ScanCandidate>` retained-best with a
`BinaryHeap<ScanCandidate>` (max-heap by score+TID-tiebreak). Replaces
`insert_visited_sorted`'s partition-point + insert + truncate pattern
with a two-case `push_retained_candidate` (push if under-full, else
swap-worst-if-better). Sorts once on return.

## What I verified against the source

- **Output is bit-for-bit identical.** `ScanCandidate::cmp` has a full
  tiebreaker chain (score → block_number → offset_number), so heap and
  vec produce the same multiset, and the final `sort()` yields the
  same ordering as the previously-maintained ascending vec.
- **Asymptotic complexity is genuinely better.** O(log K) heap ops vs
  O(K) sorted-vec insert+truncate per visit. At K = list_size = 200,
  log2(200) ≈ 8 vs ~100 expected shift cost — ~12× per insert on
  paper.
- **No new `unsafe`. No anti-pattern-B issues.**
- **Stop-threshold check is correct.** Old `next >= visited_best[list_size-1]`
  vs new `retained_best.peek().is_some_and(|w| next >= *w)` — both
  ask "is the next candidate at least as bad as the worst retained?"
  Heap peek is the max (worst) by Ord, matching the vec's last
  element semantics.

## Why I'm recommending revert/reframe

1. **No measurable performance win at the Phase 2 5% gate.**
   - L=64 total mean: −1.66% (frontier −0.85%, but exact rerank
     −4.28% which is unrelated noise eating most of the headline).
   - L=200 total mean: **+0.99%** (frontier +0.75%, rerank +1.58%).

   Both are inside per-run noise. The slice neither wins nor loses
   meaningfully.

2. **Added complexity for no payoff.** The old
   `insert_visited_sorted` was a 3-line operation (partition_point,
   insert, truncate). The new `push_retained_candidate` is a 12-line
   two-case function. For perf-neutral work, simpler-is-better
   should govern.

3. **The asymptotic argument doesn't kick in at K=200.** Cache
   locality on a contiguous sorted Vec beats heap-order access for
   small K. The constant factor of heap branches/comparisons eats
   the asymptotic win at this scale. The slice might pay off at
   K=1000+; the bench at K=200 doesn't see it.

4. **Pattern observed: packets 004, 005, 006 all chase the frontier
   residual without a sub-timing breakdown.** Packet 006's
   "rerank-result-retention" slice was already reverted (commit
   `3f3fa8bfe`). The methodology of "tweak the residual, measure,
   keep if it wins" is right, but the residual is composite — heap
   ops + visited bitset + neighbor iteration + bounded-frontier
   ordering — and without sub-timings the slice is shooting in the
   dark. **My packet 003 review point 1** asked for that sub-timing
   pass before more Phase 2 slices; that recommendation lands here
   too.

## Recommendation

- **Revert `9bbef9ecf` from the Phase 2 line.** Keep the simpler
  `Vec`+`partition_point` form from packet 004.
- **Before the next Phase 2 slice**, instrument the frontier
  residual itself: separate measurements for (a) candidate heap
  push/pop, (b) visited-bitset query/insert, (c) inner neighbor
  iteration, (d) bounded-frontier maintenance. That will identify
  which subcomponent of the 70–83% frontier share is actually the
  cost driver. Then a slice targeting that subcomponent has a
  real chance of hitting the 5% gate.
- **Alternative**: keep the slice but reframe the packet as a
  code-quality / data-structure-idiomatic cleanup, not a Phase 2
  perf slice. Then it doesn't need to clear the 5% gate. The
  tradeoff is that future readers see a BinaryHeap and assume it
  was chosen for measured reasons.

The coder's honesty in the packet ("I do not consider this to
retire the frontier P0") is exactly the disposition the workflow
expects. No demerit for the slice not winning — the discipline of
shelving non-wins is the right discipline. Just close this packet
with a revert and direct effort at the sub-timing instrumentation.

## Verdict

**Revert** (or reframe as cleanup-only with explicit non-perf
framing). Do not bank this as a Phase 2 P0 win.
