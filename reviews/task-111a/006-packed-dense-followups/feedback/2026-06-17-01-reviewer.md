## Verified correct

- **Finding 1 — single-segment borrow fast path (real, not just a counter).**
  When `segment_index == 0 && segment_count == 1`, the scan flushes the
  coalescing scratch and routes the group through
  `process_dense_posting_block(IvfDensePostingRef::PackedSegment(segment), …)` —
  i.e. it scores from the **page-resident** payload slice + typed gamma view and
  **returns early, skipping `from_header`**. So the owned-Vec assembly copy is
  genuinely avoided for page-fitting groups, and `record_dense_packed_group_borrow`
  vs `record_dense_packed_group_assembly` make the two paths distinguishable in
  EXPLAIN. Correct implementation of the lever.
- **Finding 2 — PG18 span+vacuum fixture.** `test_ec_ivf_dense_packed_typed_span
  _vacuum` covers a real multi-segment (page-spanning) group, the single-segment
  borrow tail, and deleted-row semantics after vacuum. This is the end-to-end
  read-stream coverage I asked for; its passing also confirms continuation
  tuples are visited/kept correctly during both scan assembly and vacuum.
- **Finding 4 — assembly/copy-bytes counters.** `record_dense_packed_group_assembly`
  / `_borrow` are wired and EXPLAIN-visible, so `007` can quantify the spanning
  copy cost across bit-widths. Exactly what the decision benchmark needs.
- **Packed vacuum correctness.** `bulkdelete_dense_packed_segment` marks deletes
  in the header segment's bitmap (metadata-once lives there), with checked
  `start/count/end` arithmetic and bounds-checked `heap_tids.get(start..end)`,
  only when all of a posting's heap TIDs are dead — mirroring the dense-block
  vacuum. The fixed-size bitmap keeps the rewrite size-preserving (Rewrite vs
  Keep), and continuation tuples (payload-only) are left untouched. Sound.

## Interpretive note (not a defect — shapes the B decision)

The borrow fast path removes the assembly *copy* for single-segment groups, but
it scores each such group independently (per-block), so it does **not** give
single-segment groups dense-a's wide cross-block coalescing. Packet 004 shows
dense-b emits ~7.5× more flushes than dense-a precisely because B's batch width
is capped by on-disk group size (~32) while A coalesces to ~240. The borrow
helps storage/copy, not that flush-count gap. So 006 makes B *cheaper*, not
*competitive* — whether B can ever beat A is still the `007` bit-width question.
There's an inherent tension worth stating in the 007 writeup: for a group that
fits a page you can either borrow-and-score-narrow (no copy) or copy-into-scratch
-and-coalesce-wide; 004 suggests wide wins for TQ, narrow is fine for rb1, so the
best choice is payload-size-dependent.
