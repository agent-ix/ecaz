- **The assembly-copy counter (added in 006) explains why, and it's decisive.**
  From the committed counters: dense-b copies **16.6 MB (rb2) → 32.8 MB (rb4) →

  9,222 physical segments — i.e. almost every group spans pages (borrowed groups:
  only 22 / 13 / 9). B also reads *more* posting pages than simple dense
  (segment fragmentation + per-segment headers). The copy scales with bit-width
  exactly as predicted. This is the quantified root cause of B's loss.

## The Task 111a decision (evidence-based)

Combining 004 (TQ + rb1) and 007 (rb2/4/8), the full matrix supports:

- **Adopt Approach A (scan-side coalescing)** as the dense scan path for the
  format that needs it (TurboQuant), and **keep the aligned typed-view layout** —
  dense-typed is the RaBitQ winner at rb4 and competitive elsewhere.
- **For RaBitQ, the simple one-page dense/typed layout is best**; coalescing is
  marginal and not needed because the RaBitQ kernel is cheap and the smaller
  footprint / fewer page reads dominate.
- **Abandon the page-spanning packed format (Approach B) as the durable shape.**
  It was built and measured per the operator directive (B did not "stay closed"),
  and the measurement is unambiguous: dominated on latency, storage, page reads,
  and copy bytes across every quant mode, at equal recall. Decoupling batch width
  from on-disk layout (A, in scratch) beats paying for the grouping on disk (B).

This is the "equal weight, measured" outcome — B got a fair, fully-built,
fully-benchmarked shot and lost. That's a successful resolution of the question,
not a gap.
