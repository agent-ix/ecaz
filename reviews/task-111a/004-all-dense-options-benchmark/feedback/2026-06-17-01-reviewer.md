## Verified against committed raw artifacts

- **Recall parity: confirmed.** `results.jsonl` shows all six variants identical
  at TQ 100k/np32 (recall 0.9370, NDCG 0.9966); spot-checks elsewhere match.
- **Flush widths: confirmed and revealing.** From the committed
  `latency-100k-tq-*.log` `block-kernel-counters` (np32):
  - dense-old: 521,755 flushes, width_ge32 = **0** (the regression).
  - dense-a: 21,778 flushes, width_ge32 = 21,443.
  - dense-b / dense-b-typed: 164,416 flushes, width_ge32 = 161,307.
  So dense-b **does** reach width≥32 — but emits **7.5× more flushes than
  dense-a** (164k vs 22k). At ~5.2M candidates that's ~32 postings/flush for B
  vs ~240/flush for A.

## The core finding: why B trails A (and it's structural)

dense-b reaches the SIMD floor (width≥32) but not A's efficiency, because **B
ties batch width to on-disk group size (~32), while A coalesces across blocks to
a ~240-wide scratch at scan time.** A's larger batches amortize per-flush
overhead ~7× better. A decouples batch width from on-disk layout (cheap, in
scratch); B pays for the grouping on disk — which also explains the storage
result (request reports dense-b *increases* index size vs row for TQ and vs
dense-a for RaBitQ: per-segment header + line-pointer + partial-tail overhead).

This is the measured, evidence-based version of the A-vs-B question you scoped
"equal weight": at rb1+TQ, **A dominates B** on latency, flush efficiency, and
storage, at identical recall. The packet's recommendation (A is the best option,
B not yet the better durable format) is supported by the data I checked.

## Why this is not yet the decision — the bit-width sweep is the open variable

004 covers only rb1 + TQ. Per the updated Task 111a (and the multi-page-payload
reasoning), **rb1 is the one mode that fits ≥32/page**; rb2/4/8 span pages and
stress the spanning path differently. B could close the gap (or fall further
behind) for larger payloads — that is precisely what the in-progress `007`
bit-width sweep must show. Do not finalize "adopt A, drop B" on rb1+TQ alone;
the decision benchmark needs rb2/4/8 with the per-group assembly-copy counter
(added in packet 006) reported. The packet correctly states this is not the
expanded decision benchmark — good.
