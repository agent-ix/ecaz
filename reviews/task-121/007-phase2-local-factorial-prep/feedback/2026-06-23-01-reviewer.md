## 4. Factorial grid correctness — CHANGES REQUESTED (the blocker)

The encoded grid (verified directly in `suite-phase2-local-factorial-dryrun.json`, and identically in the 008/009 slices) is:

- `training_sample_rows` ∈ {10000, 50000}

### Blocker A — `nlists` is in the grid but Stage 1 measured it NEGATIVE at fixed nprobe.

The Stage 1 100k OFAT data (`001-stage1-routing-screen/artifacts/manifest.md`) is unambiguous: at the **fixed routing budget** Phase 2 uses, finer `nlists` *monotonically lowers* route-stage containment / recall at every nprobe:

| nprobe | nlists=128 (base) | 316 | 512 | 1024 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 0.8525 | 0.8230 | 0.7630 | 0.7190 |
| 32 | 0.9310 | 0.8975 | 0.8480 | 0.8110 |
| 96 | 0.9975 | 0.9720 | 0.9350 | 0.9215 |

Stage 1's own interpretation: nlists "does not fix route-stage loss … recall is below baseline from nprobe 16 through 96 … not a standalone significant route-recall lever." 512 and 1024 confirm the trend. This is precisely the "screened NEGATIVE — hurts at fixed nprobe" finding.

The defense (in 005 re-plan and these packets) is to keep `nlists=316` as an *"interaction/cost axis,"* on the theory that finer leaves cut candidate volume/latency and may pair well with boundary replication. That theory traces back to the *interim* reviewer note (`001/.../2026-06-22-02-reviewer.md`) written **before the nlists cells landed**, which hypothesized nlists "is the one most likely to rival boundary replication" but explicitly said to "hold the significant-set verdict until nlist*/… land." Once they landed, the data refuted the hypothesis: at fixed nprobe, 316 is strictly worse for recall. The interaction theory is reasonable but **unproven**, and the prep packet does not state it as a measure-the-interaction hypothesis — it carries 316 into a 2-level factorial axis as if it were a co-equal lever, doubling the cell count (48 vs 24) on a NEGATIVE main effect.

Per the Phase-2 charter (saturate the *significant* set, exclude the dropped/negative ones), `nlists` should not be a standalone factorial axis. Two acceptable resolutions — pick one and state it in `manifest.md`:

1. **Preferred — drop `nlists` from the grid.** Fix `nlists=128` (the screen baseline / best route-recall point), halving the matrix to 24 cells and letting Phase 2 saturate `boundary_replica_count × training_sample_rows × fanout` cleanly. Faster, and matches the significant set.
2. **If you genuinely want the boundary×nlists interaction**, keep 316 but reframe it explicitly in the config `_doc` and `manifest.md` as a *cost/interaction probe with a negative main effect* — i.e. you are testing whether boundary replication recovers the recall that finer leaves give up, in exchange for lower fanout/latency. That is a defensible question, but it must be named as such, and the success criterion is a recall-vs-(latency+storage) Pareto win, not raw recall. Do not present 316 as a route-recall lever.

### Blocker B — `recursive_fanout=16` is omitted; fanout is pinned at 8.

The reviewer brief flags `recursive_fanout=16` as marginally significant and asks for it in the grid. The Stage 1 data backs a *small* low/mid-nprobe gain: fanout16 route recall is 0.8605 @nprobe16 and 0.9170 @nprobe24 vs baseline 0.8525 / 0.9045 — a few tenths of a point, at near-baseline storage and candidate volume. fanout32 did not confirm a monotonic trend (worse than 16 at most nprobe). The 005 re-plan dropped fanout entirely on the fanout32 non-confirmation.

Dropping fanout32 is fine. But pinning fanout at 8 and excluding the one value (16) that showed a cheap positive nudge means Phase 2 cannot confirm or kill it, and cannot test whether it compounds with boundary replication (the central Phase-2 question). Add `recursive_fanout ∈ {8, 16}` as a 2-level axis. It is cheap (near-baseline storage/fanout) and it is the marginal-significant lever the charter says to carry.

### Net grid recommendation

Replace `nlists ∈ {128,316}` with `recursive_fanout ∈ {8,16}` (Blocker A option 1 + Blocker B): the grid becomes `boundary_replica_count{0,1,2,4} × training_sample_rows{10k,50k} × recursive_fanout{8,16}` = 16 cells/scale, 48 total — same step budget as today but spent on the *significant* set instead of a negative lever. If the team insists on keeping the nlists interaction probe, do Blocker A option 2 *and* add fanout, but say so in `manifest.md` and accept the larger matrix.
