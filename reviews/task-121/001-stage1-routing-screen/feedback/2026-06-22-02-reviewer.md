## Finding B — beam widening is NOT a precision lever; it only unlocks more overfetch

The corrected high-nprobe test:

- `baseline` ceiling (nprobe 96, beam 96): route recall **0.9975**
- `tgsl200` (beam 200) at nprobe 128 / 160 / 200: **1.0000 / 1.0000 / 1.0000**

So a wider beam *does* reach 1.0 — but only by allowing nprobe to climb past 96,
i.e. by scanning more leaves. That is Task 120's overfetch axis extended, not a
new precision lever: it buys recall with query cost (more leaves scanned), the
same trade `bound*` buys with storage. Contrast at route recall ~1.0:
`bound2` hits 1.0000 at **nprobe 96**; `tgsl200` needs **nprobe 128** (≈33% more
leaves) to match. **Read beam-widening as "raise the overfetch ceiling," and
boundary replication as "raise recall at fixed/lower fetch."** They are not
interchangeable and should be Pareto-plotted as such.
