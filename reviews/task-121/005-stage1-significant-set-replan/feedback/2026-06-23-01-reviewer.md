## Two judgment-call notes

1. **`nlists=316` interaction is a hypothesis, not a screened-significant lever —
   acceptable to test, but flag it as speculative and gate it.** The screen shows
   316 is recall-*negative* standalone; the packet keeps it only on the
   "paired with boundary replication it may recover recall at a lower
   candidate/latency point" interaction theory. Full disclosure: that interaction
   theory originated in my own interim feedback (-01/-02), where I suggested finer
   leaves + boundary replication "attack the same centroid-as-proxy problem and
   likely compound." **The standalone screen data does not support that** —
   finer leaves lose coverage at fixed nprobe (same nprobe covers a smaller
   fraction of more leaves), which is a *subtractive* mechanism, not one that
   obviously composes favorably with replication. I am explicitly walking back the
   "likely compound" framing: it is unproven and the screen leans against it.
   The packet's framing is the defensible version of it — 316 only (not 512/1024),
   explicitly as a cost/interaction axis, not a recall winner. Keep it in the grid
   **only as a 2-level interaction probe**, and state in the Phase 2 manifest that
   the standalone screen was negative so a null/negative interaction result is the
   expected baseline, not a surprise. Do not let nlist=316 be reported as
   "promising" going in.

2. **`recursive_fanout=16` was dropped; that is defensible but state it.** The
   task Phase 2 text lists fanout among possible interactions and the prompt's
   expected ranking allowed an optional `[x fanout]` axis. fanout16 is a genuine
   (if marginal, +0.023 @np8, decaying to negative @np96) low-nprobe positive;
   fanout32 did not confirm it. Excluding it to keep the grid tractable is a
   reasonable call, but the packet should say *why* it was dropped while a
   negative-standalone lever (nlists) was kept — currently the asymmetry is
   unexplained.
