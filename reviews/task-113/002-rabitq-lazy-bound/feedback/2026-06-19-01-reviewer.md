The lazy driver's fetch closure is `|i| approx_scores[i]` — it feeds *approximate*
frontier scores, not true *exact* scores. Sound today (stop never fires: floor ==
width, slack = +inf, finite-gate) and it keeps `worst_kept` finite as intended,
but it is still a placeholder w.r.t. true exact-score feeding, and there's a real
architectural tension behind it: the tid-sorted batch rerank fetches the kept
prefix *after* the prefix length is decided, so exact scores aren't available at
plan time without breaking the locality batch. If the k-cap is ever pursued, that
tension (not just the k-cap) must be resolved. A one-line note in `lazy.rs` that
the closure currently feeds approx, not exact, scores would prevent a future
misread.
