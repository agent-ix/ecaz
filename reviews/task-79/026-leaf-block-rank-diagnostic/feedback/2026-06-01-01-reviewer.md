- 90% of winners are at rank ≤109 (well under 384)
- 95% are at rank ≤223 (still under 384)

**The diagnostic resolution**: it's not "1 budget bump away,"
it's "spread distribution of missed winners across ranks 400-1000+."
That means the summary score has a long tail of low-IP-bearing
blocks that nevertheless contain real winners, which can only be
fixed by giving each block MORE information to discriminate.

## Two failure-mode subcategories quantified

The diagnostic also reveals two separate failure modes:

1. **5 of 2000 targets are `not_found_in_routed_leaves`** — the
   routing layer drops these winners before block selection
   even runs. That's a 0.25% ceiling on recall (max 0.9975) even
   with perfect block selection — matches the Task 78 baseline.
2. **57 of 2000 targets are routed but rank below cap 384** —
   block-selection failure. This is the addressable bucket.

So at most 0.9975 recall is reachable; the gap from 0.9690 to
0.9925 is closing 47 of those 57 block-rank misses. That's
specifically a "make block scoring more discriminating" problem.

## What the diagnostic rules out

- **Wider rerank** (packet 021): already ruled out. Confirmed.
- **Small budget bump** (packet 025 R1 first branch): ruled out.
  Misses are at ranks 400-1000+.
- **Pure routing improvement**: only 0.25% misses (5/2000) live
  at the routing layer. Routing is fine.
- **Deterministic row sampling** (packets 017/020): the diagnostic
  rank distribution suggests samples are unlikely to fix it —
  blocks at rank 500-800 need to BE ranked higher, not have their
  sampled rows score well.

## What it points at

> "improve per-block information content or block-score
> discrimination at the same 384-416 candidate budget"

Coder's recommendation is correct. Two viable directions:

1. **Multi-representative summaries** (k centroids per block) —
   directly addresses information content. ~2-3 packets,
   format extension, +k× summary scoring cost.
2. **Calibrated block score** — keep one summary per block but
   improve the scoring function. E.g., learn a per-leaf
   calibration factor at build time so summaries from different
   leaves are comparable on a normalized scale. Could potentially
   fix the long tail without format change.

(2) is worth a try before (1) because it's cheaper, but harder to
predict success.
