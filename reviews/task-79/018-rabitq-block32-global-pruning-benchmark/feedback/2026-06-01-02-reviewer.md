# Task 79 packet 018 — follow-up: try wider rerank_width before Option A1

## TL;DR

**Before the next selector-side iteration (samples-as-adjustment or
multi-rep), test the rerank-width axis on the existing packet 015
global selector.** This axis hasn't been explored under block
pruning, and it's the natural mechanism IVF uses to compensate for
small candidate surfaces. Could be a one-packet test that closes
Task 79 without further selector work.

## The observation

Cross-AM comparison at the same recall band:

| AM | candidate surface | rerank_width | recall@10 | p50 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| RaBitQ baseline (T78) | 15.5M cands, 200q | 25 | 0.9975 | 60.3 ms |
| IVF control (T76) | ~75k cands/q | 500 | 0.9980 | 37.7 ms |

IVF reaches identical recall with a **much smaller candidate
surface** by compensating with a **20x wider exact rerank**. RaBitQ
baseline gets away with `rerank=25` only because 15.5M candidates
gives the noisy quantized score so much redundancy that the top 25
reranked almost always contain the true top 10.

When Task 79 packets shrink the candidate surface 3-4x via block
pruning but hold `rerank_width=25` constant, the noisy RaBitQ score
no longer has enough redundancy to deliver the right top 25 → recall
drops. That's what packets 011/013/015/017/018 all showed,
attributed to "summary semantics" — but the real binding constraint
may be **rerank_width not scaling with shrinking candidate surface**.

## What's been tested

- Packet 005 (rerank-budget-probe): tested `rerank=50` and
  `rerank=100` at n512 with route-time row budget. Maxed at 0.9840
  recall. But that combined a different geometry (n512 vs baseline
  n128) with a tight row budget — recall was constrained by both
  axes simultaneously.

## What hasn't been tested

The combination **block pruning (packet 014/015 global selector) +
wider rerank** (rerank=100/200/500). Specifically:

| selector | block pruning | rerank_width | predicted outcome |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| global384 (015) | yes | 25 | 0.9675 recall (measured) |
| global384 (015) | yes | **100** | ? |
| global384 (015) | yes | **200** | ? |
| global384 (015) | yes | **500** | ? |

If the RaBitQ noise hypothesis is right, wider rerank should
recover recall toward 0.9925+ at the same candidate count. If
recall doesn't move with wider rerank, then the summary semantics
truly are the binding constraint and Option A1/B remains the path.

## Why this is the right next packet

1. **Cheap to test.** The existing global selector (packet 014)
   plus existing `rerank_width` knob. No new code. Just a suite
   sweep with different `rerank_width` values at the global384
   /global512 settings from packet 015.
2. **Task 79 spec allows it.** "Retained and returned counts remain
   comparable, *unless the packet intentionally changes rerank
   width and proves the full recall/latency/candidate tradeoff*."
   Exactly the case where this is justified.
3. **Resolves the attribution.** Right now packets 011/013/015/017/018
   all attribute the gap to "summary information content." That's
   a plausible but unverified inference. The wider-rerank test
   either confirms it (no recall recovery → summary semantics is
   the wall) or refutes it (recall recovers → rerank narrowness
   was the wall).
4. **Could close Task 79 directly.** If RaBitQ at global384 with
   `rerank_width=200` reaches recall 0.9925+ and p50 stays under
   45ms, that's a complete Task 79 closure recipe with **zero new
   code** — just GUC tuning.

## Cost model

Wider rerank means more exact f32 distance computations per query.
At rerank=200 vs 25, that's 175 extra exact distances per query.
Each exact distance is ~768 multiply-adds; at modern CPU
throughput that's microseconds per row.

200 × 175 × 200 queries = 7M extra exact distance computations
total. At ~1µs each, ~7 seconds total → ~35ms/query overhead
(rough upper bound; actual may be less due to vectorization). At
current global384 p50 of 43.2ms, adding ~5-15ms for wider rerank
keeps it under 60ms baseline and possibly under the 45ms gate
depending on how SIMD-vectorized the rerank kernel is.

Worth one packet to find out.

## Recommendation

Before Option A1 or Option B, run **one packet**:

- selector: global block pruning (packet 014 code)
- block geometry: block64 (packet 015's best)
- final blocks: 384, 512 (the two near-gate rows from 015)
- **rerank_width sweep: 25 (baseline), 50, 100, 200, 500**
- record: recall@10, p50, p95, candidate_sum, retained_sum (which
  will scale with rerank), returned_sum

If recall passes the gate at any (final_blocks, rerank_width) row
with p50 ≤ 45ms or ≤ 25% better than baseline, Task 79 closes.

If recall doesn't recover at any rerank width tested, the
attribution to "summary information content" is confirmed and
Option A1/B is the path.

Either outcome resolves the open question with one packet.
