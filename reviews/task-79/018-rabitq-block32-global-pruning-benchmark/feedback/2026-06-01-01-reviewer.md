# Task 79 packet 018 — block32 global pruning benchmark

## Decision

**Accept as honest negative evidence.** Smaller blocks (32 rows
instead of 64) give finer granularity but still don't clear the
recall gate within the candidate budget. The block-geometry axis is
now exhausted at the no-format-change layer.

## Comparison to block64 (packet 015)

| candidates | block64 (015) recall | block32 (018) recall |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ~4.7M | 0.9675 (global384) | 0.9520 (global512) |
| ~6.3-6.4M | 0.9860 (global512) | 0.9730 (global768) |
| ~8.0-9.4M | 0.9925 (global768) | 0.9920 (global1280) |

Block32 is **worse** than block64 at every comparable candidate
count. That's an interesting and informative result:

- Smaller blocks = more summary scoring CPU (4x more summaries at
  block32 vs block128, 2x vs block64)
- Smaller blocks = less spatial averaging in the mean → noisier
  per-summary signal
- The noisier summary signal more than offsets the finer granularity

So the geometry axis at the summary-only layer has a sweet spot
near block64 — going smaller doesn't help. That eliminates a
trivial-to-test hypothesis cleanly.
