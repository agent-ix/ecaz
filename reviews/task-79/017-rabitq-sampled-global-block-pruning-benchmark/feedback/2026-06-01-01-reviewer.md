# Task 79 packet 017 — sampled global block pruning benchmark

## Decision

**Accept as honest negative evidence.** Sampling is *worse* than
summary-only at comparable candidate counts — the sample score is too
noisy to replace the summary score, and replacing it discards the
already-validated global-allocation signal from packet 015.

The closeout's diagnostic insight — "preserve the summary score as a
prior and let samples adjust it instead of replacing it" — is exactly
right. The current selector overweights the noisy sample.

## Direct comparison

| approach | candidates | p50 | recall | source |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| summary-only global384 | 4.68M | 43.3 ms | 0.9675 | packet 015 |
| sampled global384/p1024/s2 | 5.10M | 49.1 ms | **0.8985** | this packet |

Sampling cost +0.42M candidates and +5.8ms p50 to **lose 6.9pp
recall**. That's straightforwardly worse on every axis. The
mechanism: replacing the summary score with k=1-2 sampled row scores
discards information. A k=2 sample of a 64-row block is noisy enough
that high-summary blocks containing actual winners get re-ranked
below low-summary blocks where the 2 samples happen to score well.

## What the `returned_sum < 2000` observation means

> "It also under-returns in several sampled rows (`returned_sum`
> below 2000 in the production read profile), which is another
> symptom of bad block selection."

That's important. With 200 queries × top-k=10 = 2000 expected
returns. Falling below means some queries' bounded heap never
fills — the selected blocks didn't contain enough candidates to
rerank to k=10. This is a real correctness/quality symptom, not
just a recall metric blip.

Good catch by the coder. Worth a note in any future packet that
sampling rendered some queries incomplete.

## Finding

### F1 — The proposed fix ("samples adjust prior") is the right next try

The closeout names two next moves:

1. **Summary-as-prior with sample adjustment** (small change)
   — keep summary score, add sample score as a residual or
   weighted update. e.g. `final_score = α × summary_score +
   (1-α) × max_sample_score` with α≈0.7-0.9 (trust summary
   heavily, let strong samples bump). Stays in V3 format.
2. **Richer per-block summaries / multi-representatives**
   (V3 extension or V4 bump). Most likely to actually close
   the recall gap per packets 011/013/015 attribution.

Recommend (1) as the next try because it's a small selector
change that directly addresses *why* this packet failed (sample
replaced summary instead of adjusting it). If (1) succeeds,
great. If (1) also fails, that's evidence the summary information
content itself is the wall and (2) is needed.

The coder might already know this — the recommendation lists it
first. Worth one more iteration before falling back to format
changes.

### F2 — Disposition of packet 016's code if (1) supersedes it

If the next iteration replaces the sample-as-replacement logic
with sample-as-adjustment, the existing GUCs
(`leaf_block_pruning_global_probe_blocks`,
`leaf_block_pruning_sample_rows_per_block`) can stay with new
semantics. The disabled-by-default contract means existing
behavior is unaffected either way.

If the next iteration goes to multi-rep instead, then packet 016's
sampling path is dead surface. Worth deciding at slice time
whether to remove it or document it as a diagnostic.

## Verdict

Negative-evidence packet accepted. The next slice should preserve
summary-as-prior and use sampling as adjustment. Coder's
recommendation matches this.
