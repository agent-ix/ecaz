# Task 79 packet 025 — clustered block64 radius-weight benchmark

## Decision

**Accept as negative-but-strongest-yet evidence.** Clustering +
partial radius (weight=0.25) is the **best summary-based result
in the entire 25-packet sequence**, but still ~0.9pp short of the
recall gate. The coder's diagnostic recommendation
(instrument missed-winners by summary-score rank) is the right
next move before any further sweeping.

## Direct improvement quantified

Comparison of clustered vs unclustered at matched candidate
counts:

| approach | candidates | recall@10 | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| unclustered mean-only g384 (015) | 4.68M | 0.9675 | baseline |
| clustered mean-only g384 (025) | 4.76M | 0.9690 | +0.15pp |
| clustered w=0.25 g384 (025) | 4.80M | 0.9760 | **+0.85pp** |
| clustered w=0.25 g416 (025) | 5.20M | 0.9835 | **+1.60pp** |

Two findings:

1. **Clustering alone is small** (+0.15pp) — the mean of a cluster
   isn't dramatically more informative than the mean of arbitrary
   contiguous rows.
2. **Clustering + partial radius is substantial** (+0.85-1.6pp) —
   the radius bound, which was useless on arbitrary blocks
   (packet 023 was *worse* than mean), becomes useful on clustered
   blocks because the radius now represents a coherent cluster
   bound, not a noisy upper limit on random rows.

That's a real win — radius-bound scoring is now usable. But the
combined improvement closes ~60% of the gap from 0.9675 to 0.9925,
not 100%.

## The remaining ~0.9pp gap

Best near-gate row: g416/w=0.25 = 5.20M cands (1% over), 45.8ms
p50 (0.8ms over), 0.9835 recall (0.9pp short).

This is "tantalizingly close on all three gates simultaneously" —
all three misses are within 1-2% of the threshold. But all three
gates are still missed.

The recall miss is the binding constraint. Coder's diagnosis is
sharp:

> "diagnose missed winners: for each query, record the summary-
> score rank of blocks containing true top-10 neighbors, then
> either calibrate the block score or add a safer two-stage
> selector."

That's exactly the right diagnostic. If true-neighbor-bearing
blocks rank at positions 400-500 (just outside the 384 budget),
a small budget bump closes the gap. If they rank at 800+, summary
scoring is fundamentally too lossy and multi-rep (or a different
selector entirely) is needed.

## What the data already suggests

Looking at the recall climb from g384 to g416 to g512 at w=0.25:
- g384: 0.9760 → g416: 0.9835 → g512: 0.9870
- That's +0.75pp/32 blocks → +0.35pp/96 blocks
- The marginal recall per added block is *falling*, not rising

Extrapolation: g480 might reach ~0.99, g560 might reach ~0.992,
g640+ would reach the gate. But those budgets push 6.5M+ cands —
over the candidate gate by ~25%.

So the geometry suggests: **with clustered + partial radius,
recall closure requires going past the candidate gate.** The
right summary-based fix is "rank better at the same budget," not
"add budget." That's what multi-rep would aim for (more info per
block = better ranking discrimination at same block count).

## Recommendations

In order of strength:

### R1 — Instrument missed-winners (coder's recommendation, do first)

One short diagnostic packet:
- Run g384/w=0.25 with the funnel logging extended to record
  "summary rank of blocks containing true top-10 vec_ids"
- If ranks cluster around 400-500: bump budget slightly and
  Task 79 closes (low cost)
- If ranks spread to 800-1500: summary signal is fundamentally
  insufficient → multi-rep is needed
- If ranks scatter randomly: clustering didn't actually help
  organize blocks by relevance → re-examine the clustering
  approach

Cost: ~1 packet of diagnostic instrumentation + one sweep.

### R2 — If diagnostic says "summary too lossy," combine D + multi-rep

Per my earlier framing: multi-rep at k=2 with clustered blocks
might cleanly close the gap. Cost: ~2-3 packets, format
extension, ~+1.3ms p50.

### R3 — Pause is still defensible

After 25 packets, the candidate-surface problem is exhaustively
characterized. Best summary-based recipe gets within 1pp of all
three gates simultaneously. That's a strong design-space-closure
artifact even without Task 79 formally closing.

## Verdict

Negative-evidence packet accepted as the strongest summary-based
result. Coder's diagnostic recommendation is the right next slice
— short, cheap, and resolves "are we 1 budget bump away or
fundamentally short?" cleanly. After diagnostic, the multi-
rep-vs-pause decision will be much sharper.

The 25-packet sequence has now produced:
- precise design-space attribution
- a working selector that closes ~60% of the recall gap
- diagnostic infrastructure to localize the remaining gap

That's substantial progress even if Task 79 doesn't close.
