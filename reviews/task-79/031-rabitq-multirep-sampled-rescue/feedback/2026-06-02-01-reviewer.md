## Why sampling continues to fail

This is now the third confirmation (017/020/031) that
deterministic row sampling doesn't add discriminating signal at
the high-recall band. Whether on single-rep or multi-rep, sampled
row scores carry too little information to identify the right
blocks.

The reason — same as I noted on packet 020 — is structural:
sample rows score *the actual rows in a block*, but the recall
gate requires identifying *blocks containing rows that will rank
in top-10 after exact rerank*. A high-IP sample row doesn't
necessarily mean the block contains a top-10 winner; a low-IP
sample row doesn't necessarily mean the block doesn't. Sampling
just isn't a good proxy for block-level winnership at this
recall band.

## What the next slice should target

Per packet 030 feedback: the 11 missed targets at cap640 are
spread across some rank range past 640. The fix needs to:

1. Lift those specific targets' summary score (so they survive
   to cap640), or
2. Reorder blocks at the final cap (so 11 lower-rank blocks
   containing winners replace 11 higher-rank blocks without
   winners).

(1) requires better per-block scoring — either k=3 reps or
per-leaf calibration.

(2) requires identifying the "right" 11 blocks to swap, which
requires per-block diagnostic data the system doesn't yet
collect.

(1) is the cheaper path. My packet 030 suggestion stands:
percentile distribution data from a follow-up rank diagnostic
will tell us if the 11 misses cluster (tight fix possible) or
spread (need stronger ranking).

## Verdict

Negative evidence accepted. Sampling is now definitively ruled
out across single-rep and multi-rep. The remaining viable paths
are score-calibration or higher-k multi-rep, not sample-based
rescue. Coder's recommendation aligns.
