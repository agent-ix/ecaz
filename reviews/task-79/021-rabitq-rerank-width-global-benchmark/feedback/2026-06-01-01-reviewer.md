# Task 79 packet 021 — rerank-width global benchmark

## Decision

**Accept as critical-attribution negative evidence.** This was my
recommended test from packet 018 seq-02, and the result refutes my
hypothesis cleanly. Recall is **completely flat across rerank
25→500** at both global384 and global512. That's a sharper
diagnostic than I expected.

## What this proves

If wider rerank could recover dropped winners, recall would climb
with rerank_width. It doesn't:

| setting | rerank=25 | rerank=100 | rerank=500 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| global384 | 0.9675 | 0.9675 | 0.9675 |
| global512 | 0.9860 | 0.9860 | 0.9860 |

Recall identical to four decimal places. That means **the winners
aren't in the candidate pool at all** — the global block selector
is dropping the blocks that contain them *before* anything reaches
the exact reranker. Wider rerank can only re-examine candidates
that made it into the pool; if they were filtered out at the
selector, no rerank width helps.

This refutes the "noisy RaBitQ score + narrow rerank" hypothesis.
The binding constraint really is **block admission**: the summary-
only global selector is excluding too many high-recall blocks at
the block-budget threshold.

## What this implies for the next slice

Coder's read in the recommendation is correct:

> "the summary-only global block cap is dropping true-neighbor-
> bearing blocks before the heap reranker sees them. The next
> implementation slice should directly address candidate surface
> admission, preserving high-recall blocks before enforcing a
> smaller global cap."

That's exactly what packet 022 implements (radius-bound scorer in
the global selector). The 022 fix may or may not work, but it's
attacking the right surface.

## Side observation: p50 cost of wider rerank is real

| setting | rerank=25 → 500 p50 delta |
| --- | ---: |
| global384 | 45.1 → 62.0 ms (+37%) |
| global512 | 48.5 → 67.4 ms (+39%) |

So rerank=500 alone adds ~17-19ms p50 on top of the scan. That's a
useful cost calibration for any future packet that wants to
combine wider rerank with anything else.

## Verdict

Negative evidence packet accepted with strong attribution: block
admission is the binding constraint, not rerank narrowness. The
packet was cheap and resolved a real open question. Glad we ran
it.
