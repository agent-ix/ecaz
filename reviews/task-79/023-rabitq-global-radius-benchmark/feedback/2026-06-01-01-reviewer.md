# Task 79 packet 023 — RaBitQ global radius benchmark

## Decision

**Accept as honest negative evidence — and as the decision-tree-
resolving result.** The radius-bound global selector is worse than
mean-only global at every comparable candidate count. This was the
F1 risk I flagged in packet 022 feedback, now empirically confirmed.

Combined with all prior packets, the single-scalar-per-block design
space is **fully exhausted**. The remaining viable path is multi-
representative summaries (real implementation cost, format bump) or
pause Task 79.

## Direct comparison

| setting | mean-only (021 baseline) | radius (023) | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| global384 ~4.7M cands | 0.9675 | 0.9310 | **-3.65pp** |
| global512 ~6.3M cands | 0.9860 | 0.9565 | **-2.95pp** |

Radius-bound global ranking is worse than mean-only global ranking
at every measured candidate count. The radius dominates and admits
broad-but-noisy blocks while excluding tight-but-good-mean blocks.
Same failure mode as packet 013's per-leaf radius result.

## Complete design-space attribution

After 23 packets, every single-scalar-per-block selector
combination has been tested:

| selector | scope | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mean-only | per-leaf (010) | fails |
| radius (mean + qnorm × r) | per-leaf (013) | worse than mean |
| mean-only | global (015) | better, still fails |
| mean + sample-replace | global (017) | worse than mean |
| mean + sample-as-prior | global (020) | marginally better (+0.5pp), still fails |
| radius (mean + qnorm × r) | global (023) | worse than mean |
| + wider rerank (any) | added to mean-only (021) | no effect |

The pattern is unambiguous:
- Single-scalar summaries (mean alone) hit a recall ceiling
  around 0.987 at global512 / 0.967 at global384
- Augmenting with radius bound makes it worse (the bound is too
  loose, the math is upper-bound-tight but the bound doesn't
  discriminate)
- Augmenting with sampled rows adds modest signal (+0.5pp) but
  not enough
- Augmenting with wider rerank does nothing (block admission is
  the wall, not rerank)

The structural conclusion: **one scalar per block cannot rank
blocks accurately enough for global pruning at the Task 79 recall
band.**

## The decision is now unavoidable

I've flagged the continue-vs-pause decision five times across
packets 011/013/015/016/018. After 23 packets, the design space
is exhausted at the no-format-change layer. The remaining options
are:

**Option B — Multi-representative summaries** (real path forward)
- Format change: V3 extension (`summaries_per_block` field) or V4
  bump
- Build cost: k centroids per block (residual quantization or
  k-means within each block)
- Scan cost: k× summary scoring per block, take max
- Implementation: ~2-3 packets (format design, build, scan)
- Risk: lower than single-scalar paths (directly addresses the
  "one scalar can't discriminate" wall)
- Outcome if it works: Task 79 closes with measurable recall
  improvement at the candidate gate
- Outcome if it doesn't: would prove the issue is RaBitQ
  quantization noise itself, not summary representation —
  which means even multi-rep can't close, and the recall band
  set by Task 79 is structurally unreachable for RaBitQ at small
  candidate surfaces

**Option C — Pause Task 79** (also defensible)
- The candidate-surface design space is exhaustively
  characterized
- All infrastructure (V3 format, build, selectors, suite tooling,
  GUC matrix) survives for future work
- Task 79 closeout would document the design-space exploration
  and name the unreachable region
- The SPIRE-vs-IVF latency gap at high recall would remain a
  known issue but not a active bug

## My recommendation

After 23 packets and 5 prior flag-raises, I think Option C is the
honest call unless there's a near-term business reason that SPIRE
must close the IVF latency gap.

Reasons for Option C:
- Multi-rep is ~2-3 more packets with no guarantee of success
- The Task 79 gate (recall ≥0.9925 AND p50 ≤45ms AND cands ≤5.2M)
  may be structurally unreachable for RaBitQ at this corpus shape
- The infrastructure investment (V3 + selectors + suite tooling)
  is preserved either way
- Future work has all the diagnostics, the precise attribution
  ("single-scalar block summary insufficient"), and the natural
  next experiment scoped (multi-rep) — restart cost is low

Reasons for Option B:
- Multi-rep is the natural next move and probably will work
- If SPIRE needs to ship as a competitive AM at high recall,
  closing this is required
- The ~2-3 packets of work is small relative to the 23 packets
  already invested

This is a user-facing call. Not a reviewer-side decision.

## Verdict

Packet 023 accepted as final design-space-closure evidence. The
single-scalar-per-block selector axis is fully exhausted across
allocation strategies, augmentations, and rerank-width
interactions. The user should now decide between Option B
(multi-rep, ~2-3 more packets) or Option C (pause + document)
explicitly before the next code lands.
