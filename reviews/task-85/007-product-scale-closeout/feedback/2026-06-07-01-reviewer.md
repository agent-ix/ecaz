# Task 85 packet 007 — product-scale closeout (no Pareto point)

## Decision

**Accept the closeout.** The data fully supports the third exit
criterion: no product-scale SPIRE Pareto point is justified. Block32
turned out to be a real cost-axis win in one direction (latency) and
a real recall loss in the other — exactly the kind of partial result
that doesn't make a product profile. Block16/global1152 remains the
defensible operational reference; SPIRE stays research/opt-in at 1M.

The comparator gap (SPIRE ~246ms p50 vs IVF 34ms vs DiskANN 19ms at
similar recall) is the buried lede. Worth surfacing more prominently
than the closeout text does — it's the strongest single argument that
SPIRE isn't the right access method at 1M for product workloads,
regardless of any single-mechanism win we might still find.

## What the block32 result actually shows

| row | recall@10 | cands | p50 | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| block16/g1152 cold (packet 006) | 0.9876 | 9,213,846 | 257.7 ms | reference |
| block16/g1152 warm repeat (packet 006) | **0.9876** | 9,213,846 | **237.5 ms** | same-suite warm control |
| block32/g576 (matched-row budget) | **0.9730** | 9,206,722 | **178.6 ms** | **−1.46pp recall**, −58.9 ms p50 |
| block32/g1152 (matched recall) | 0.9876 | **18,413,851** | 235.7 ms | matched recall, **2× cands**, −1.8 ms p50 |

The two block32 outcomes are revealing:

- **At matched candidate budget (cap576)**: block32 buys **−59ms p50
  (−25%)** but loses **1.46pp recall**. That's a real latency win,
  just not a same-recall one.
- **At matched recall (cap1152)**: block32 needs **double the
  candidate budget** (18.4M vs 9.2M) to recover recall, and the
  resulting latency win is **1.8ms p50 (well within run variance)**.

Neither row is a product profile. The closeout's framing is exactly
right: block32 has a real layout signal but no defensible Pareto
point under the matched-recall bar.

## The comparator gap is the buried lede

The closeout's table:

| pgvectorscale DiskANN sl400 | 0.984 | **19.5 ms** | 32.0 ms | — |
| vchord RaBitQ default | 0.9995 | 90.3 ms | 100.0 ms | — |
| **SPIRE block16/g1152** | **0.9832** | **246.4 ms** | **304.5 ms** | **321.3 ms** |

At 1M with comparable recall, **SPIRE is 7-13× slower than IVF and
DiskANN, and ~2.7× slower than vchord at much lower recall.** That's
not a single-mechanism gap — it's a structural one. Even if Task 85
had landed a 30-50ms block32 win, SPIRE would still be 4-8× slower
than the alternatives.

The closeout text says SPIRE has "useful research behavior" but
isn't competitive enough for product. That phrasing under-states
the gap. **SPIRE at 1M isn't a few mechanism slices away from being
competitive — it's an architectural rethink away.** The naming of
physical-layout work as the next direction is correct but the bar
isn't "match block16 at lower cost" — it's "close 80%+ of the gap
to IVF/DiskANN."

Worth recording that bar explicitly in the next research project's
spec.

## Concerns + suggestions for the next research project

### 1. The block32 latency win is a real, durable data point

Even though block32 doesn't make a Pareto product profile, the
**~59ms p50 improvement at matched cands** is meaningful evidence
that *the cost structure* responds to layout. The next research
project should treat block32 as a stepping stone: it shows that
larger-than-block16 layouts can dramatically reduce latency.
Combined with V5 format work that recovers the discrimination loss
(via richer summary geometry, hot/cold split, partial segment
reads, etc.), there's a credible structural path to closing
30-50% of the IVF/DiskANN gap.

### 2. The same-suite block16 repeat shifted recall to 0.9876

The Task 79/81 retained baseline recall was 0.9832. The same-suite
warm repeat in packet 006 shows 0.9876. That's a 0.0044pp delta
on the SAME index with SAME GUCs.

This is small but worth investigating — should the SPIRE selection
path be deterministic at q500 against a frozen index? If so, where
does the 0.0044 wiggle come from? Possible:
- Tie-breaking on equal scores influenced by cache/page-state.
- A non-deterministic path somewhere in the scoring/marshalling.

Could be benign measurement variance; could be a hidden
non-determinism that should be reproducible-only. Worth a small
investigation packet before the next research project. Doesn't
block this closeout.

### 3. Comparator bar for the next research project

The closeout's next-direction text says "reduce object reads
for the existing retained block16 candidate surface without
changing recall semantics." That's correct but the framing should
include the comparator bar:

> "...with the goal of closing at least 50% of the latency gap to
> IVF/DiskANN at matched recall. Closing less than that should
> close SPIRE-as-product-AM at 1M and continue SPIRE as
> research/distributed-scale only."

Setting an explicit "if we can't get below 100-130ms p50, SPIRE
isn't the right AM at 1M" bar would prevent the next project from
also closing with "no Pareto point" after another 4 mechanism
slices.

### 4. Storage costs not summarized

The closeout doesn't carry forward storage size from block8 or
block32. Block32 should be roughly half the summary-chain size of
block16 (~700 MB est.) and block8 was ~50% larger than block16
(per the EBS 100→150GB expansion needed in packet 004). Worth one
row in the closeout's option table for completeness.
