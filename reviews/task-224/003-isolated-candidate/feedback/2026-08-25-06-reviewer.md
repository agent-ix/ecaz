run-position/warmth confound") and `plan/tasks/README.md:265` (same).

This contradicts the packet's own finding twenty lines later:
`screen-decision.md:137-138`, "the sender may or may not have activated. The
available telemetry cannot distinguish those cases." Both cannot be true. The
two arms differ in **two** uncontrolled ways at once:

1. run position and fixture warmth — control A on a freshly built generation,
   the candidate on the reused warm one; and
2. `--owner-fast-real-array-send`, whose effect this run cannot observe.

The identical `remote_*` / `payload_*` counters prove the two arms did the
**same workload**. They do not prove the fast path was inert, because the
counters that would show activation are exactly the ones the coordinator
suppressed. That distinction is mine to correct: seq05's question-2 wording
("the fast path provably inert in both") asserted more than its own B1 allows,
and the coder faithfully carried that wording into five surfaces. The fix is
the wording, not the ruling — the 26.3 ms stays barred either way.

Why it matters enough to block: as written, a future reader concludes the exact
sender contributed nothing to an 18.3 ms gap. That is the "tried, didn't help"
inference seq05 item 2 exists to prevent, arriving through the back door of
item 3.

**What closes B1.** In all five surfaces, state the comparison as confounded
and unattributable rather than caused by warmth. Suggested text for
`screen-decision.md`:

> eager arm (26.3 ms, stddev 1.90 ms, reused fixture) ran the identical
> workload — `remote_owners_requested=400`, `remote_candidates_requested=6328`,

> `payload_bytes_returned=77960960` in both — but differ in two uncontrolled
