## Artifact 1: prediction byte-parity is not a candidate property

The request lists "Prediction SHA parity: **FAIL**" in the candidate's column for
50k pair B and both 100k pairs, and concludes "The candidate fails semantic
parity."

I hashed the prediction files. At **50k**:

```
pair-a/rowheap-first    1abc27ffe21b97f0
pair-a/hotcold-second   1abc27ffe21b97f0
pair-b/hotcold-first    1abc27ffe21b97f0
pair-b/rowheap-second   1d5139fa0dafce5c   <- the outlier is a CONTROL
```

Three of four arms are byte-identical **across both layouts**, and the one that
diverges is row-heap. At **100k**, all four differ — including
`pair-a/rowheap-first` (`02dcd617…`) against `pair-b/rowheap-second`
(`667bfe30…`), which are two identically-configured **control** arms.

Two controls cannot disagree with each other because of the candidate. So
prediction byte-parity is measuring **run-to-run reproducibility of the harness**,
and it degrades with scale: exact at 10k, 3/4 at 50k, none at 100k. It is not a
candidate gate at 50k or 100k and must not be cited as one.

This one matters beyond bookkeeping. Byte-identical predictions were the
strongest semantic-parity check available, and I said so when I authorized the
preregistration. Learning that it is unmeasurable at the decision scale is a real
limitation of this evidence and belongs in the record as such.


The candidate is **0.9275 in both pairs** — perfectly reproducible — while the

## The finding that is missing, and it is the important one

Look at which arm is unstable.

- **Controls at 100k: 12.40 ms and 8.67 ms** — a 43% spread between two
  identically-configured row-heap arms.
- **Candidates at 100k: 9.47 ms and 9.47 ms** — identical to three significant
  figures, in *opposite* counterbalance positions.

Pair A's 23.6% "win" and pair B's 9.2% "loss" are both driven almost entirely by
control variance. The candidate produced the same number regardless of position.

That is exactly what the counterbalanced design was built to expose, and what it
exposed is not "hot/cold is slower." It is: **hot/cold delivers stable ~9.47 ms
irrespective of position, while row-heap ranges 8.67–12.40 ms depending on
warmth.** The candidate beats a cold control decisively and loses to a warm one.

The mechanism explains it. The PLAIN inline tier has no TOAST pages to warm, so
there is nothing for it to gain from a prior arm; row-heap's performance depends
heavily on TOAST residency, so it is fast once warm and slow when not.

Under the frozen rule this is still STOP — the candidate must beat its matched
control in both pairs and it does not. I am not proposing to override the gate.
But Tasks 231 and 232 inherit whatever conclusion this packet records, and
"hot/cold is slower" would be the wrong one to hand them. "Hot/cold trades peak
warm latency for position-insensitivity, and loses against a warm control" is
what the data says.
