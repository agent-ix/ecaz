## Finding 1 — storage denominators, closed

Two gates now, each with a stated referent:

**Per-tier.** Candidate hot main-heap bytes ≤ **1.35×** `raw_vector_bytes`, both
sides referring to the indexed exact vector. I checked the derivation:
8192 / 6144 = **1.3333**, so the gate sits about 1.2% above the predicted value.

That margin is thin on purpose and it is correct to be thin. The hot heap holds
exactly one row per 8 KiB page, so its size is `rows × 8192` regardless of the
`vec_id`, identity, scalar, header and line pointer — all of which live inside
the same page. Even counting FSM and visibility-map forks the ratio only reaches
about 1.334. So this gate **can actually fail** if PLAIN amplification costs more
than predicted, which is exactly what a gate calibrated to its own hypothesis
should do, and what the original 1.35× whole-generation rule could not.

**Whole-generation.** ≤ **1.15×** control, with the arithmetic spelled out:
≈ 819 MB PLAIN hot pages − ≈ 633 MB control TOAST storage + ≈ 4 MB tuple and
locator overhead ≈ **+190 MB** on 2,498 MB ≈ **1.08×**. That matches my own
figures, and 1.15× leaves roughly seven points of margin while still catching an
unexplained whole-generation expansion.

Two gates with two denominators, each testing a different failure: the per-tier
gate tests the prediction, the total gate catches the gross defect. That is what
I asked for.

## Finding 2 — prediction accounting, closed and extended

New §5 classifies every §6 timing prediction as supported or falsified
independent of its numeric guardrail, and states the case I raised explicitly:
"A flat or worse exact-vector result is therefore explicitly a falsified
prediction even when it stays inside the 5%/0.25 ms guardrail."

It also covers the direction I did **not** name, and that omission was mine:

> Conversely, an unexpectedly neutral or faster cold/mixed/all result is also
> reported as falsified rather than silently rewritten as success.

That is the more insidious case. A cold-only arm that comes back faster than
predicted is a falsified model of the system, and the temptation would be to
present it as a bonus win rather than as evidence the cost model is wrong. Ruling
it out in advance is better than what I asked for.

The separation is clean: "Prediction classification is part of the decision
record; the numeric gates above still determine PROMOTE versus STOP." Predictions
are the epistemic accounting, gates are the decision. Keeping them distinct is
what stops a surprise in either direction from being retrofitted into the verdict.

## One observation, not a request

§5 defines neutrality as exact equality of `elapsed_ms / iterations`. Real
measurements are never exactly equal, so in practice there is no neutral band and
any strictly-higher exact-vector result — including one 0.001 ms above control —
classifies as falsified.

I am not asking for a change. The strict definition makes "supported" harder to
claim, which is the conservative direction for a decision packet, and the
classification does not gate anything. Worth knowing when reading the resulting
table so a noise-level falsification is not over-read.
