## The cross-tier invariant is the right one

Making every write-fault snapshot layout-aware, and requiring that **hot and cold
tuple totals stay equal before, during, and after 2PC recovery**, is exactly the
invariant a hot/cold fault matrix needs. A partial write that left a hot tuple
without its cold counterpart — or a recovery that replayed one side twice — is
the characteristic failure of a two-relation write path, and nothing else in the
suite would catch it. Emitting `cold_pair_balanced=true` on the result line makes
it auditable from the artifact rather than implied by the absence of an error.

Reading hot identity from compact `a_2` and requiring the cold tier's presence
for this arm are both correct for the fixture schema, where `source_id` (attnum
2) is the identity.

The retained-generation read is well-placed: after successor publication and
predecessor retirement but **before** reclaim, which is the only window where
retained-predecessor reads are observable at all. Requesting attnums 1 and 3
together forces genuine hot-plus-cold reconstruction (`id` is hot, `source` is
the cold array), and asserting `Retired` admission alongside the four-relation
catalog shape ties the read to the lifecycle state rather than just to a row
count.

Counting `cold_tier_relid` so hot/cold requires four live relations where
row-heap requires three propagates the layout distinction into the Task 235
status reader correctly.

## To reconcile: payload offsets arity

Two surfaces appear to disagree about the length of `payload_offsets`, and I
could not resolve it from the code alone.

This checkpoint asserts, for a two-attnum request:

```sql
ARRAY[1,3]::smallint[] ... bool_and(array_length(payload_offsets, 1) = 3)
```

— two attnums, three offsets. But the packet-002 seq-10 pgrx tests assert:

```rust
null_materialize("2,5")     -> offsets == vec![0, 0]        // 2 attnums, 2 offsets
null_materialize("1,2,4,5") -> offsets.len() == 4           // 4 attnums, 4 offsets
assert_eq!(usize::try_from(offsets[3]).unwrap(), values.len());
```

Both suites pass, so they cannot be checking the same function under the same
convention. Either these are different entry points with different encodings —
in which case say so, because a reader comparing the two artifacts will assume
otherwise — or one of the two assertions is checking the wrong arity, and an
off-by-one in offsets is precisely the class of reconstruction bug that produces
correct-looking rows with shifted attribute boundaries.

I flagged the offset-advance behaviour as the thing worth pinning back at
seq-10 and I still think so; this is the same contract seen from the SQL side.
Please state which arity is authoritative for
`ec_distann_materialize_physical_row_payloads` and make both suites assert it the
same way. Not blocking — both suites are green and I have no evidence of an
actual defect — but it should be settled before packet 004 cites either.
