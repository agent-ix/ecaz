## Note: the orphan columns will be non-zero for healthy generations

`cold_orphan_row_count = cold_row_count.saturating_sub(colocated_row_count)`
(`handoff.rs:2112`) mirrors the existing hot-side definition at
`handoff.rs:2111` exactly, so this checkpoint is consistent rather than novel.
But the definition interacts with a contract clause that this packet has not
exercised yet.

Packet-001 §5: a same-identity replacement appends a new cold/hot pair and a new
graph version, and **predecessor tuples remain for snapshot-pinned readers until
generation reclaim**. After one replacement, a perfectly healthy generation has
two hot rows and two cold rows against one current graph record — so both
`orphan_row_count` and `cold_tier_orphan_row_count` report 1.

Nothing is wrong in the code. The risk is interpretive, and it lands in two
places still ahead:

- **Retained-predecessor coverage**, still owed in this packet, will be the
  first test to produce non-zero orphan counts on a healthy generation. Decide
  now whether the assertion expects that, or whether the definition should
  exclude retained predecessors.
- **Packet 004**, which reads topology and storage numbers per arm. A non-zero
  "orphan" count on a candidate arm that ran DML would read as corruption to
  anyone who did not trace the definition.

Either document the semantic where the columns are defined, or subtract retained
predecessors from the orphan count. My preference is documenting it — the raw
difference is the more useful number, provided nobody mistakes it for a fault
signal.
