## Both gaps closed

**1. V1 writers now reject a cold locator**
(`distann_physical_node_v1_writers_reject_cold_locator`, `tuple.rs:775-786`).
It sets a *valid* `cold_tid` on an otherwise-good `sample()` and asserts both
`encode_physical_v1` and the legacy `encode` fail.

I traced both paths to confirm the test reaches the guards rather than tripping
something earlier. `encode_physical_v1` → `encode_with_version(.., Some(1))` →
`validate_physical_v1` → base `validate` passes → `heap_tid` valid → the
`cold_tid.is_some()` guard at `tuple.rs:172`. `encode` →
`encode_with_version(.., None)` → base `validate` passes → the guard at
`tuple.rs:257`. Delete either guard and this test fails, which is what the
seq-03 finding was about.

**2. Pooled V2 decode and V2→V1 reuse are exercised**
(`distann_physical_node_decode_into_reuse_clears_v2_cold_locator_for_v1`,
`tuple.rs:912-944`). It calls `decode_into_physical_version` for V2 into a
`placeholder` buffer, asserts the `cold_tid` is populated, then decodes a V1
record into the *same* buffer and asserts `cold_tid == None`.

The reuse assertion is the one that matters and it is pinned correctly: remove
`out.cold_tid = None` at `tuple.rs:445` and the pooled tuple carries a stale
locator into the V1 decode, failing the assert. The trailing
`assert_eq!(pooled, v1_tuple)` is a good addition beyond what I asked — it
confirms the whole reused record, not just the one field, matches a freshly
encoded V1 tuple. It also gives `decode_into_physical_version` its first
exercise on both versions.
