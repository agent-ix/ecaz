## Decision-soundness notes (independent of the diagnostic issue)

### D1 — Unexplained mechanism: nprobe -> recall when candidates are saturated

The decision text observes:

> the tg64/tg96 comparison shows that equal candidate counts do not
> imply equal recall (`0.9825` vs `0.9975`)

…but doesn't propose a mechanism. If two settings score the same
candidate pool and the same number of winners per leaf
(`candidate_winner_count = 25` every query at both tg64 and tg96 per
the funnel JSONL), what changes recall? Possibilities:

- Per-leaf `nprobe`/winner allocation differs by leaf even when the
  aggregate winner count is constant — i.e. tg96 distributes the 25
  winner slots across leaves differently from tg64.
- The candidate pool isn't actually the same — the diagnostic SQL is
  reporting the same numbers, but the production scan visits different
  rows (the cross-channel mismatch in packet 001 supports this).
- Rerank ordering or tie-breaking differs.

Whatever the mechanism is, the Phase 2 decision is incomplete without
identifying it. If the mechanism is "production scan visits different
rows than the diagnostic reports," that's another way the diagnostic
needs fixing.

### D2 — "Score-bound early termination" shelve rationale is sound

The correctness proof needed for pushing a heap bound into approximate
per-leaf quantized scoring is real work and a one-shot diagnostic packet
isn't the place to land it. Agreed shelve.

### D3 — "Tighter recursive draft" shelve rationale is conditional

The reasoning ("does not identify a routing-level predicate that can
reject those leaves before scoring without changing recursion
semantics") depends on the funnel data being correct. If the fixed
diagnostic shows per-query leaf-set variation, there may well be a
routing-level predicate visible (e.g. "leaves whose centroid distance
to query exceeds X never produce winners"). Reopen after the fix.
