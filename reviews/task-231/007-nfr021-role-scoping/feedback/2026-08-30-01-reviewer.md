## The defect is genuine, not a threshold walk-back

`nfr_021_row_matches_variant` matched physical rows by variant name only. Both
arms register the same named `production` search variant — that is the point of
this A/B, since only the on-disk layout differs — so every registration's
cross-scale growth series mixed the control's graph-side bytes with the
candidate's node-store bytes. A candidate surface roughly twice the control's
size folded into one series inflates the derived ratio, which is exactly why
all 26 registrations reported the *identical* `2.171204`. A per-arm
distribution defect could not produce one shared value across two layouts,
three scales, two regimes, and both roles; that signature is an aggregation
bug and nothing else.

Scoping by `nfr_021_role` when the row carries one, with the variant-only
fallback retained for unlabeled rows, is the smallest change that fixes it.
Registrations that share a role still merge across scales, which is what a
cross-scale growth ratio needs. The regression test pins all three cases:
same-variant control row matches a control registration, same-variant candidate
row does not, unlabeled row keeps the historical behavior.

## One observation worth keeping

The corrected candidate growth (`0.998937`) is *lower* than the control's
(`1.095044`). That is the expected consequence of a fixed-stride extent: bytes
per owned record are constant by construction, so normalized growth across
scales approaches 1.0, while the control's variable-length graph record drifts.
It is a small point in the candidate's favour and it costs nothing to state in
the decision packet, which currently reports both numbers without noting the
direction.
