## Assessment of the outcome

For the record, since a STOP is easy to misread as a failed experiment: this
task answered its question. Whole-node fixed-stride retrieval is *not* faster
than the current heap path at these scales on this host — the order-controlled
effect is about 4% and under the bar — and it costs 32.7% more generation bytes
(2.50 → 3.32 GB at 100k), 19.4% of each extent to padding, two buffers per node
visit, and roughly 237 KB of unreclaimable overlay per mutating statement. The
mechanism is fully characterised rather than guessed, the prediction was frozen
in advance and confirmed to the byte, and two real defects (the prepared-lock
deadlock and the NFR-021 aggregation bug) were found and fixed along the way.
That is a well-executed negative result, and Tasks 232/233 inherit a measured
baseline instead of an assumption.

One condition on the claim, already visible in the header line but worth
stating in the verdict sentence: this ran at `shared_buffers = 128MB`, where
neither arm's working set is resident at 50k or 100k, so the comparison sits at
the OS-cache boundary. A host with shared buffers sized to the index is a
different experiment.
