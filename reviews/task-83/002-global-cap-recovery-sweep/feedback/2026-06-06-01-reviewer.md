# Task 83 packet 002 — global-cap recovery sweep + closeout

## Decision

**Accept the closeout.** The sweep correctly measures that raising
cap recovers recall monotonically, and correctly rejects this as the
policy because it materially inflates the candidate surface that
Tasks 79/81 spent the entire arc reducing. Recommending selective
recovery / better block scoring for the next slice is the right call.

## The data is honest

| cap | recall@10 | candidates | cands delta | p50 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1152 (baseline) | 0.9832 | 9,213,846 | — | 288.769 ms |

Cross-check: each +128 cap adds about +1.0M candidates over 500q =
+256 candidates/query × 8 rows/block = ~2k extra rows, matching the
expected geometry. Numbers are self-consistent.

Recall recovery per +1M candidates:
- 1152→1280: +0.14pp recall per +1M cands
- 1280→1536: +0.15pp per +1M cands
- 1536→1664: +0.16pp per +1M cands

**Diminishing returns are very mild** — that's notable. It means the
remaining misses ARE recoverable by cap inflation; they're just not
recoverable at a *favorable* rate vs the latency cost.

## Why this rejects "blanket cap" correctly

To recover all 81 selected-leaf misses to 0.9994 (3 routing misses
still remaining), you'd need roughly:
- Linear extrapolation: ~+0.16pp per +1M cands × ~1.6pp gap = ~10M
  additional cands → cap ≈ 2,400 → ~19M candidates total = roughly
  back to the Task 78 baseline (15.5M) the entire arc moved away from.

So blanket-cap recovery is structurally a *reversal* of the Task 79
work, just with the V4/deferred-decode plumbing in place. Closeout
correctly names this.

## Recommended next slice scope is correctly bounded

Two paths named in the closeout:

1. **Improve selected-block scoring** so true target blocks rank inside
   1152. The Task 84 enriched diagnostic gives the calibration data
   needed for this.
2. **Bounded rescue path** for ambiguous near-cap leaves/queries —
   spends extra budget only where the score margin is small.

Both are valid. The Task 84 packet 001 enriched data suggests:
- 26 of 81 misses are within 0.01 score margin → calibration-recoverable
- 28 of 81 are in top-8 routes → route-aware scoring might help
- 55 of 81 are at -0.01 or worse margin → these likely need (1), not (2)

So the realistic mechanism is probably (1) for the 50+ "bigger gap"
misses plus optional (2) for the ~30 near-cap misses. A pure (2)-only
rescue would be capped at ~30 misses → max recall ~0.992. A pure
(1) approach could in principle address all 81, given enough
calibration signal.
