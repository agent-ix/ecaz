## Finding 1 (Minor, clarity): "scalar" lane is not the same comparator as packet 017

This is the one thing worth fixing in the request prose before it gets
cited elsewhere. The packet 017 "no-scratch scalar baseline" was, per its
own framing, no-scratch-SoA + scalar SIMD. Packet 021's `scalar` lane has
`ivf_scratch_soa_batch_decode: true` in *both* suite configs — the only
axis between scalar and auto here is `--simd-mode`, not the SoA path.

That is consistent with what the request actually says
("scratch-SoA scalar and auto are nearly identical … 1.13 vs 1.15 …"),
and the "speedup vs packet 017 scalar" framing is fine *because* the
delta combines scratch-SoA + auto SIMD versus no-scratch + scalar.

Recommend the request explicitly label that the 2.86x number is the
**combined** scratch-SoA + auto-SIMD improvement over no-scratch scalar,
not a SoA-only delta. The pure SoA effect is roughly packet 017 scalar
(2.28/3.70/6.57 ms) vs packet 021 scalar (1.13/1.53/2.33 ms), i.e.
~2.0x / 2.4x / 2.8x at the scalar level, with auto-SIMD adding only the
~0–6% extra seen in the auto/scalar column. Worth stating so packet 020
(5.59x raw kernel) and packet 021 (≈1.0x SIMD-over-scalar at SQL level)
aren't read as contradictory.

## Finding 2 (Advisory): interpretation framing is sound

The request's conclusion — "the remaining gap is above/around the
scan/query path rather than the raw kernel loop" — is consistent with:

- packet 020 measuring 5.59x kernel speedup in isolation,
- packet 021 measuring ≈1.0x SIMD speedup at SQL level once SoA is on,
- recall identical across lanes and identical to packet 017.

That is the right takeaway and the right next step (ec_ivf scan-path
work, not another kernel microbench). No reviewer pushback on direction.

## Recommendation

Accept packet 021 as evidence that:

1. scratch-SoA is a real, recall-neutral SQL-level win for bits=1 at
   10k/real-Intel — ~2x at nprobe=16 up to ~2.86x at nprobe=64 over the
   packet-017 no-scratch scalar baseline;
2. once SoA is on, scalar vs auto SIMD at the SQL level is within
   ±6% — i.e. the AVX-512 bits=1 kernel measured in packet 020 is *not*
   the remaining bottleneck for total wall time;
3. the Task 67 3x headline gate is **not** met by SoA alone, and the next
   slice must target `ec_ivf` scan-path overhead (candidate recording,
   batched min-bound pruning, or an explicit redefinition of the gate as
   kernel-throughput rather than SQL wall-time, with reviewer agreement).

Leave the request open for the coder to optionally add the Finding 1
clarification before any downstream packet cites the 2.86x as a
SoA-only number.
