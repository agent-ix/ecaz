## The expensive anti-pattern: reloading 100k from scratch every attempt

Each representative attempt **re-provisions a fresh cluster and reloads the full
100k corpus from zero** at ~38 rows/s (~40+ minutes of load) before it can even
reach the bench. That is the "no recreate corpus" rule inverted: the loaded
representative corpus + built SPIRE index should be **snapshotted once** and
**restored** on subsequent attempts (`ecaz cloud up --from-snapshot` style),
not rebuilt every cycle. This is why iteration is so slow/costly and why several
attempts have died mid-load. Recommend: get one successful representative
load+build, snapshot it, and drive bench iterations off the restored snapshot.

Also: ~38 rows/s for SPIRE build on real 1536-dim data is itself worth a look —
at 1M (the eventual representative target) that's ~7+ hours of load. The
per-insert build cost may need batching/parallel build before 1M is feasible.
