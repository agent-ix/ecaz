## P1-1 — Phase 1 answered a different question than the task asked

Task 188 Phase 1 requires six attributions. Three ran (head-vs-oracle seed
comparison, isolated BW, isolated H). Three did not:

- (3) candidate-frontier and exact-rerank **containment of truth neighbors**;
- (4) components, indegree, bridge structure, **hard-query reachability**;
- (5) **monolithic graph quality vs shard closure/stitch effects**.

Build time and graph bytes (6) appear only as unchanged storage. What was run is
a beam-width/hop-round sweep — precisely the "undifferentiated parameter sweep"
the Goal section tells the task to avoid, and the exact reason Phase 2 says
"pre-register at most one candidate **from the attributed dominant family**". No
family was attributed, so `GRAPH-06`–`GRAPH-09` (build), `GRAPH-10`–`GRAPH-13`/
`GRAPH-16` (connectivity/stitch) and `GRAPH-14`/`GRAPH-15` were never in
contention; BW8 won by being the only thing measured.

Quantitatively: the bounded head sits at 0.9740 and the owner oracle at 0.9970
under identical search. BW8 recovers 0.0065 of that 0.0230 gap. **~72% of the
residual is left unattributed**, and packet 004 never discharges the task's

## P1-3 — no scale shows a CI-separated recall gain, and the wrong test is being used

10k is at the ceiling (both 1.0000) so it carries no information; the request's
"recall-neutral at 10k" is not a null result, it is an uninformative cell. At 50k
and 100k the CIs overlap heavily; +0.0065 at 100k is **13 of 2,000 trials** and
+0.0025 at 50k is **5 of 2,000**. Packet 002's "clear recall improvement" is not
supported by the instrument reported.

The fix is not more queries, it is the right test. BW4 and BW8 share the
generation, the query set, and — per the packet's own attestation — the exact
seed digest
`26daaa06e76426faeb151329efc77f04c9fb44684ce81600055e13955b154bcd`. This is a
**paired** comparison; report McNemar or a paired bootstrap over the shared 2,000
trials, which is both the correct instrument and far more powerful than the
unpaired CIs currently cited. Please also report per-query win/loss counts —
"BW8 wins on N queries, loses on M" is the decision-grade form of this claim.

## P1-4 — every Task 188 arm ran unbatched materialization; the "flat mean latency" is partly an artifact

All 188 arms report `materialization_batch_size=0`. Tasks 185 and 186 ran at 10.
This is not in the configs — it is a CLI defaulting asymmetry in
`crates/ecaz-cli/src/commands/dev/distann_multicluster.rs`:

- the no-variant path constructs the `production` variant with
  `materialization_batch_size: 10` (~line 3855);
- `parse_benchmark_seed_variants` defaults the field to `0` when it is omitted
  (~line 1191), and `materialization_variant_settings_sql` then emits
  `SET ec_distann.benchmark_materialization_batch_size = 0` (~line 1334).

So adding `benchmark_seed_variants` to a step silently switches the run out of
the batched (extension-default) regime. That is exactly what happened between
186 and 188, and it is why 186's cap-16,384/BW4/H100 arm is **27.10 ms** while
188's identical-configuration BW4 control is **43.40 ms** — roughly 16 ms of
constant unbatched materialization cost sits in every 188 latency number.

Two consequences: (a) 188's absolute latencies are not comparable to 185/186's
and packet 001 cross-cites them as if they were; (b) the constant inflates the
denominator, so BW8's "+0.70 ms, essentially unchanged" at 100k understates its
relative traversal cost — under batch=10 the same absolute delta would be a much
larger fraction. Re-run the A/B at `materialization_batch_size: 10` before any
acceptance decision, and consider making an unset variant field inherit 10 so the
regime cannot flip silently.
