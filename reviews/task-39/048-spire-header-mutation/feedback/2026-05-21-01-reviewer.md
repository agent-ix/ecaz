## Findings

### 1. The claim "35/35 killed with no new tests" is sound

`header.rs` is small (127 lines), tightly factored into encode +
decode round-trip + identity rejection. Every mutation operates
on one of three guard predicates or one of three body
arrangements; the test surface comprehensively covers both
paths.

### 2. Round-trip-centric test design pays off

This packet illustrates the value of round-trip + dedicated-
identity-rejection patterns. Together they form a strong base
for any wire-format file: any body change breaks round-trip;
any guard change breaks identity rejection. Future codec
mutation campaigns will likely follow the same shape.

### 3. The `kind` and `flags` fields are wire-format constants without dedicated guards

Worth noting (non-blocking): the `kind: u8` and `flags: u32`
fields are encoded directly without any validity guard. A
mutation that flips a kind enum byte or sets an invalid flags
bit would only be caught by the downstream consumer
(`SpirePartitionObjectKind::decode` for kind). For flags, no
validity check exists in this file. That's a coverage gap in
the *source*, not a mutation gap — flags don't have a
validator to mutate. Out of scope for this review.
