## Two observations worth recording

1. **The TID-sorted advantage shrinks at c=4 vs c=1 in absolute
   ratio for f16.** At c=1 (packet 016): random-id ~17–18 ms,
   tid-sorted ~0.9–1.4 ms (12-20× ratio). At c=4: random-id 34.7
   ms, tid-sorted 18.7 ms (1.9× ratio). The tid-sorted advantage
   compressed by ~10×. Plausible explanation: under contention,
   the sequential TID-scan benefit gets interleaved with other
   queries' scans, and the prefetch/locality win partially
   collapses. **This is exactly the Graviton question** — does
   the TID-sorted product path actually hold up under realistic
   concurrent load, or does it converge toward random-id behavior?
2. **Random-id also gets worse under c=4** (~17 → 34.7 ms, 2×).
   That's mostly expected cache pressure. The ratio collapse
   above is the more interesting signal.

These are reasons to keep the Graviton 1m concurrency measurement
on the asks list (016 ask 4 carries forward) rather than treating
it as answered by the local smoke.
