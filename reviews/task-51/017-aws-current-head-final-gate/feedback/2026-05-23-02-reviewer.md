## What the sidecar Graviton cells actually answer

The 016 feedback's load-bearing question was "does the TID-sorted
advantage hold at 1m on Graviton." The answer is **yes, and better
than the local 50k smoke suggested**:

| Cell | Local 50k (016) | AWS 1m (017) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| f16 TID-sorted c1 | ~1.4 ms | **0.523 ms** |
| rabitq8 TID-sorted c1 | ~0.9 ms | **0.413 ms** |
| f16 random-id c1 | ~17 ms | 18.76 ms |
| rabitq8 random-id c1 | not measured | 1.918 ms |

TID-sorted sidecar I/O on Graviton at 1m is *faster* than on local
WSL2 at 50k. That's the opposite of what I anticipated when 016
landed — the working assumption was Graviton would be slower in
absolute terms. The TID-sorted path benefits from m8g's actual
sequential I/O behavior more than the local fixture predicted.

The product implication: TID-sorted sidecar at 1m total-bound p50 is
~43.5 ms vs the no-sidecar baseline 69.1 ms — about **37% projected
latency reduction at high recall**, with sidecar storage of 1.43 GiB
(rabitq8) or 2.83 GiB (f16) on top of a 298 MB ec_ivf index. That
is the real Task 51 finding, even though it ships as follow-up code,
not Task 51 code.

## Tail behavior is the concerning signal

The headlines don't show this without looking, but the sidecar p95/p99
columns tell a story the closeout should not gloss over:

| Cell | p50 | p95 | p99 | Tail ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| f16 random-id c1 | 18.76 ms | 324.07 ms | 529.69 ms | ~28× |
| rabitq8 TID-sorted c1 | 0.41 ms | 0.44 ms | 0.54 ms | clean |
| rabitq8 TID-sorted c4 | 1.12 ms | 1.72 ms | **334.87 ms** | ~300× |

Two distinct failure modes:

1. **Random-id has catastrophic p99 tails even at c=1.** ~28× spread
   p50→p99. This is what cache thrashing under random-id lookup looks
   like on a 1m corpus. It's evidence that random-id sidecar isn't
   a viable product path even before concurrency is added.
2. **TID-sorted is clean at c=1 but blows up at c=4** specifically in
   p99 (300× spread). p50/p95 hold up well; the outlier is in the
   long tail. This matches the c=1 → c=4 ratio compression seen
   locally in 022, except worse in p99 than I expected.

For the product follow-up, the c=4 p99 spike is the more interesting
failure mode — it suggests the TID-sorted optimistic case has a
hidden concurrency interference path (perhaps locking, perhaps a
page-cache cliff). Diagnosing that is on the critical path for any
sidecar product feature.
