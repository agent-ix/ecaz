## Substantive observations on the numbers

| Threshold | recall@10 | recall_p10 | p50 vs static |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| static p=64 | 0.9570 | 0.8900 | — |
| gap=1000 | 0.9490 | 0.8000 | **−22%** but recall tanks |
| gap=10000 | 0.9570 | 0.8900 | +5% (no win) |
| gap=100000 | 0.9570 | 0.8900 | ~flat |

The signal is: the only threshold that moves latency is the one that
also kills recall. Conservative thresholds match static probing
because the policy never fires. That suggests the **gap metric is the
wrong knob** for this data, not just that the threshold is poorly
tuned.

The plan lists alternative signals — "centroid score margins,
candidate score margins, or observed bound progress." Score-gap-in-µs
of centroid scan is one of those, but probably the weakest. Worth
trying:

- centroid score *ratio* (top-1 vs top-N margin), independent of
  absolute scan time
- bound-progress signal (how quickly the candidate frontier stops
  improving) — closer to what vchord-like adaptive paths actually use
