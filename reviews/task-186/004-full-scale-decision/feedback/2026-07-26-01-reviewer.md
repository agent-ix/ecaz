## Verdict

**REQUEST CHANGES.** The capacity arm is a clean, well-provenanced run and the
"do not promote" outcome is reasonable. The hierarchy arm is not decision-grade:
it screened a Task 181 prototype whose latency is dominated by per-query work a
real two-level design does at build time, and whose representatives are
arbitrary. The pre-registered compressed arm was never built or run. Two of the
three required screen arms are therefore unresolved, and the task-level STOP is
stronger than the evidence supports.

## P1-1 — the hierarchy latency result measures the prototype, not the design

`search_hierarchy` (`head_sample.rs`, "Task 181 pre-registered two-level bounded
hierarchy") rebuilds its region index **on every query**:

```rust
let mut regions = BTreeMap::<u8, Vec<usize>>::new();
for (node, vector) in self.vectors.iter().enumerate() {
    regions.entry((benchmark_geometry_region(vector) >> 4) as u8)
        .or_default().push(node);
}
```

At cap 16,384 that is 16,384 region computations plus a `BTreeMap` of 256
`Vec<usize>` allocated and dropped per query, before any routing happens. The
scoring it then does is ≤256 representatives + ≤512 second-level = **≤768 inner
products, ~21× fewer than the exact arm's 16,384** — yet the arm measures
84.30 ms mean against the exact arm's 27.10 ms. The hierarchy is 3.1× slower
while doing 21× less scoring work. That gap is the per-query bucketing, not the
routing design. Region assignment is a pure function of the stored vector and
belongs in the head build.

Packet 002's conclusion ("loses both recall and latency despite using the same
stored head capacity") and packet 003's rejection both rest on this number.
Either re-run with build-time region assignment, or restate the STOP as scoped
to this prototype.

## P1-2 — the representatives and the second level are strawmen

Same function:

- the first-level representative for each region is `nodes.first()` — the
  arbitrary lowest-indexed member in insertion order, not a centroid or medoid,
  so first-level routing is close to a random probe of each region;
- the second level walks the 16 opened regions in ranked order and pushes members
  until it hits the hard cap of 512, so if the top-ranked region has ≥512
  members the other 15 opened regions contribute **nothing** — the "open 16
  regions" cap is not what actually happens;
- the caps 256/16/512 are hardcoded constants with no sensitivity arm.

Recall 0.9440 is a property of that construction. It is not evidence that a
two-level representative/group design cannot match a 16,384 exact head.

## P2-1 — "useful monotonic improvement" is not supported by the reported instrument

Re-derived from `results.jsonl`:

| arm | recall | 95% CI | warm mean | head cache |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| cap 4,096 | 0.9625 | 0.9532–0.9700 | 19.70 ms | 25,892,203 B |
| cap 8,192 | 0.9690 | 0.9605–0.9757 | 22.40 ms | 51,791,241 B |
| cap 16,384 | 0.9740 | 0.9661–0.9801 | 27.10 ms | 103,582,519 B |

Every pair of CIs overlaps, including 4,096 vs 16,384 (0.9661 < 0.9700). More
telling: the **single-index comparator, identically configured in all three
arms**, measured 0.9260 / 0.9120 / 0.9290 — a 0.017 spread across rebuilds of a
surface the experiment does not touch. That empirical noise floor is larger than
the 8,192 gain (+0.0065) and comparable to the 16,384 gain (+0.0115).

The 8,192 arm was the pre-registered gate for running 16,384 ("only if the 8,192
control shows a useful monotonic recall signal"), so this matters procedurally,
not just rhetorically. What *does* support the direction is the monotone
coverage trend — `zero_fraction` 0.015 → 0.005 → 0 and `owner_membership_rate`
0.5503 → 0.5733 → 0.6155 — which is a mechanism measurement, not a noisy
endpoint. Please lead with that and, for recall, use a paired per-query test
over the shared 2,000 trials (McNemar / paired bootstrap) rather than unpaired
CIs; the arms share queries, so the paired instrument is both correct and much
more powerful.

## P2-2 — packet 001's two arms and the 16,384 arm came from different suite runs

The manifest is upfront that 4,096/8,192 ran into `run-benchmark-feature` and
16,384 ran separately with `--only` into `run-cap16384` about 40 minutes later.
That is acceptable, but combined with the rebuild variance above it means the
three-point "trend" spans three independent generations
(2,496,651,264 / 2,496,659,456 / 2,496,634,880 bytes). Say so where the trend is
cited.

## What is solid (verified, no action)

- **The hierarchy arm isolates routing from membership correctly.** The
  hierarchy step and the cap-16,384 exact step report the *same*
  `head_sample_digest` `b64271e764ec6b4c5f75e3a9e60824a62627fd72d3c23f112a204af004643179`,
  same `head_sample_bytes` (101,122,048) and `head_graph_bytes` (2,460,471).
  Only `seed_strategy` differs. That is exactly the right control and it was
  achieved across separate suite runs — good discipline.
- **Train/eval disjointness is real and enforced in code**, not just claimed:
  `load_query_split` at `head_sample.rs` takes rows 201–400 for training while
  evaluation is rows 1–200, and every `physical_benchmark_landmark` row carries
  `evaluation_prefix=rows_1_200`, `training_prefix=rows_201_400`, and the
  training-slice SHA-256.
- Topology gate (3 owners, 2 remote verified, 100,000 source rows, zero
  non-owned/orphans) and engagement pass in every arm; storage, head bytes and
  construction time are all reported; the suite configs are checked in and the
  results log matches `results.jsonl` line for line.
- No production default, format, or code path changed. Correct for a screen.

evidence it is not.
