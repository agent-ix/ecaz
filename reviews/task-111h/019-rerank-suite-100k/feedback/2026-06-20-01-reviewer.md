# 111h — Warm-cache rerank-format suites trustworthy (packets 017/018/019); matched-recall analysis still the gating gap

Audit of the three benchmark packets (`017` 10k pilot, `018` 50k, `019` 100k),
answering the review asks in each: are these sufficient warm-cache evidence, and
is the interpretation strictly supported by the packet-local artifacts? I
spot-checked headline numbers against the committed `results-report.jsonl`,
verified methodology against each `manifest.md` + `suite-config.json`, and
checked the cross-format conclusions against the raw rows.

## Verdict: trustworthy warm-cache evidence; interpretation honest and not overclaimed

### Data integrity — PASS (all packets)
Every cited nprobe=32 cell I spot-checked (recall@10, p50, ec_ivf index size,
B/row, total size) across source-f32 / index-f16 / rabitq4 / rabitq8 / turboquant
at all four widths traces **exactly** to `results-report.jsonl`, in all three
packets. Latency-range key lines match too, including the honestly-flagged 100k
outlier (`index f16 w256 @ np32`: 13.8 ms p50 but 211.8 ms max-query — correctly
reported as an outlier, not p50) and the turboquant block-kernel candidate/elapsed
counters. No cited number was unlocatable or in disagreement.

### Methodology — PASS (all packets)
- Driven by checked-in `ecaz bench suite` configs (81 steps = precheck + 20
  cells × load/recall/latency/storage), **20 isolated prefixes** (one table+index
  per format×width — satisfies the index-isolation rule), standard sweep
  `nprobe 8/16/32/64/128/200` × widths `32/64/128/256` × all five formats.
- **Release `.so` confirmed** — build logs show `Finished release profile`, and
  the installed `ecaz.so` sha256 matches the manifest across all three packets.
  The known `cargo pgrx test` debug-overlay trap was avoided; latency ran on the
  release build.
- Corpus SHAs recorded and match the live `data/staged-current/ec_real_{10,50,100}k`
  staged files (real DBpedia). 018's manifest honestly discloses the unrelated
  worktree formatting-only edits present during build and confirms the backend
  hash still matched 017 — correctly handled.

### Interpretation — supported, not overstated
- **"index f16 ≈ source f32 recall, ~14× index storage": supported.** f16 tracks
  f32 within ~0.0005–0.001 recall at every matched cell; index size ~13–14×
  (342 vs 24.6 MiB at 100k). f16 is recall-neutral but storage-prohibitive.
- **"rabitq4/8/turboquant lose substantial recall": supported, and it *grows*
  with scale.** nprobe200/w256 ceiling gap vs f32 is ~0.02 at 10k but ~0.05–0.06
  at 100k. Not overstated.
- **Overall lean: 0/96 index-side cells beat source f32 on recall** at any
  matched (width, nprobe) in any packet. Source f32 is simultaneously the recall
  ceiling and the smallest index. The packets do not claim a compact-format win.
- Trend is monotone and non-contradictory across 10k→50k→100k.

## The one substantive gap before a promote/iterate/abandon decision

**P2 — No matched-recall (or matched-latency) cross-format comparison.** Both
Task 111h ("final decision table comparing … at matched recall") and 111g
(recall ≈0.97/0.99 matched baselines) ask for formats lined up at equal recall to
compare latency/storage. These packets present **only same-nprobe rows** plus
per-format recall ceilings — they never put f16/rabitq/TQ next to f32 at a fixed
recall target. That is exactly the analysis the closeout decision turns on (e.g.
"to reach recall 0.97, what does each format cost in latency/bytes?"). The packets
don't claim to have done it, so it's an honest omission, not an error — but it is
the next required step, and it's mostly a re-slice of the data you already have
in `results-report.jsonl` (no re-run needed for the warm-cache scales).

## Bottom line
These three packets are decision-grade warm-cache evidence and the
interpretation is strictly supported. On this slice the data promotes **source
f32** as the recall/storage reference, marks **index f16** recall-neutral but
storage-prohibitive (~14× — iterate on the layout before adoption), and shows the
**compressed formats lose real, scale-growing recall**. To convert this into a
111h closeout you still need: the matched-recall table (P2, from existing data),
the 1M scale (with confirmed isolation), and at least one cold/remote sweep for
the surviving candidate(s). Task stays open on those — but this is strong,
honest progress.
