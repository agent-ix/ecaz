## The unexplained gain, and why it does not block

Candidate measures **faster** at every scale (+14.40% at 50k, +9.13% at 100k,
+16.48% at 10k). The bounded wrapper only adds work, so this direction has no
mechanism behind it. The packet says so plainly and declines to claim the
improvement. That is the correct call and I want to credit it — banking a
favourable phantom is how Task 234's `-6.37%` happened.

The reason it does not block signoff is that the drift is bounded by an
in-run reference the earlier attempts did not have. Each fixture also measures
a monolithic single-node control table in the same cluster at the same moment:

| Scale | `control_rows_per_second`, control arm | candidate arm | drift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10k | 7.353 | 7.077 | +3.90% |
| 50k | 1.147 | 1.156 | +0.78% |
| 100k | 0.579 | 0.593 | +2.42% |

At the 50k decision scale the host reference moved **0.78%** while the
distributed arm moved **14.40%**. Host-level drift between the two arms is
therefore small, and the residual is something in the distributed fixture that
this design cannot separate. I am recording it as unexplained rather than
pretending it is understood.

It does not threaten the conclusion because the conclusion is a
**non-inferiority** claim. The question seq-01 asked was "what does the bounded
write wrapper cost, and is that cost accepted." The measured cost is zero or
negative at both resolving scales, with the favourable margin an order of
magnitude larger than the host-drift canary. A cost that cannot be detected
under an instrument this stable at 50k (control CV 1.70%) is not a cost the
program needs to accept — it is an absence.

One honest caveat for the record: the 10k candidate measured 0.8469 at 13:31
and 1.0112 at 23:35 on the same configuration, a +19% swing. That is the
instrument's drift floor at 10k, and it exceeds the 50k signal. It is precisely
why 10k carries nothing here and why I moved the decision scale to 50k in
seq-07. The preregistration held up.
