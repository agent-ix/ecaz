was the cheapest scale. With 50k now in hand, that reasoning is wrong:

| step | arm | mean rows/s | CV | 95% CI half-width, n=5 |

| control-50k | physical | 0.5072 | **1.70%** | **±2.11%** |
| control-50k | single-node | 1.1330 | 3.54% | ±4.39% |

**50k physical is five times tighter than 10k physical.** At ±2.11% it already
resolves well inside the 7.1%–17.5% band Task 234 measured for the read-side
wrapper. No extra trials are needed to answer Task 235's question.

10k is the noisy one, not the cheap one. That is consistent with its mechanics:
at 0.868 rows/s a 32-row trial is ~37 s, so fixture warm-up and scheduling
jitter occupy a larger share of each measurement than they do at 50k's ~63 s.
Buying resolution at the noisiest scale was exactly backwards.
