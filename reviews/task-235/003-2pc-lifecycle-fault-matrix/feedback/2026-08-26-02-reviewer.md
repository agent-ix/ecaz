## The resolution problem, stated before it happens

As configured, each arm at each scale produces **one aggregate ratio from 160
rows** (`TASK167_AB_TRIALS = 5` × `TASK167_AB_ROWS_PER_TRIAL = 32`,
`distann_multicluster.rs:3704-3705`). I checked whether dispersion could be
recovered afterwards from what the runner writes: it cannot. The summary log
carries `trials=5` and a single `physical_rows_per_second`; there are no
per-trial timings, no stddev, and no per-trial rows in any committed artifact.
So the packet will land with six numbers and no way to say how much of any
control-versus-candidate difference is run-to-run variation.

That is precisely the shape Task 234 landed in. Its packet 004 reported the
candidate 6.37% *faster* at 50k from single observations; packet 005's repeat
then measured an 8.2% spread across three runs of the *same* control SHA and
reversed the sign. Three review rounds and two extra benchmark runs, all to
recover a dispersion estimate that could have been collected the first time.

The write path is more exposed to this than the read path was, not less. At
0.766 rows/sec a 160-row sample is roughly 3.5 minutes of wall clock per arm,
single-row inserts through a three-owner 2PC round — a workload with
per-transaction fsync and lock-wait variance that the read benchmark's 50 warm
iterations do not have.

## Three ways to close it, cheapest first

1. **Emit per-trial timings.** The runner already loops
   `TASK167_AB_TRIALS` times; recording each trial's elapsed time alongside the
   aggregate makes dispersion recoverable from the artifact with no extra run
   time at all. This is the best option — it is a few lines in the emitter and
   it fixes the metric permanently for Tasks 229–233, which will all need write
   evidence.
2. **Raise `TASK167_AB_TRIALS`** for this suite. More samples per arm, linear
   cost, but still yields no dispersion figure unless combined with (1).
3. **Add a repeat step per arm**, the way Task 234's packet 005 did with
   `reuse_fixture: true`. Proven to work on this fixture, but it is the most
   expensive of the three and it re-measures rather than instruments.

Option 1 alone is sufficient and is the one I would take. If the 10k and 50k
control steps have already run under the current emitter, re-running just those
two after the change is far cheaper than discovering the gap at review.
