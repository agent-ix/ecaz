## Verdict

LGTM as comparative evidence, and the three conclusions are well-supported by the
committed numbers: carry **f16**, reject **rabitq8** for the high-recall path,
and prefer **tid-sorted** table-side reads. The packet correctly flags its own
caveat so the absolute latencies aren't over-read.

## Verified / agree

- **f16 carried:** at k50 and k100, f16 matches f32 recall/NDCG (0.9940 / 0.9960)
  at half the sidecar bytes (146 vs 293 MiB; 150 vs 300 KiB touched p50). A clean
  Pareto improvement for table-side rerank — right call.
- **rabitq8 rejected for the high-recall path:** much smaller/faster (73.8 MiB,
  1.2–2.3 ms) but recall@10 drops to 0.9460. Correctly excluded from the
  immediate high-recall target; it stays a candidate for a recall-relaxed mode.
- **tid-sorted >> random-id is the big lever:** k100 p50 IO 33.7→3.0 ms (f32),
  53.0→1.9 ms (f16), 31.7→1.2 ms (rabitq8). The placement/read-order finding is
  the most actionable result here — random-id heap reads dominate p50 even at
  50–100 candidates, so any table-side rerank must read tid-sorted.

## Notes

1. **Honor the caveat downstream.** The candidate-SQL p50 in this packet
   (~251–272 ms) is an order of magnitude off the packet-001/006 frontier runs,
   so these are *comparative* numbers for representation + read-order only, not
   end-to-end latency. The closeout should state that explicitly so the f16/
   tid-sorted decision isn't mistaken for a latency result.
2. f16 isn't yet in the contract's `rerank_format` enum (003 has f32/heap_f32/
   rabitq2/4/8/turboquant). If f16 is the carried table-side representation, the
   contract surface needs an `f16` value when that implementation slice lands —
   flag for the follow-up.
