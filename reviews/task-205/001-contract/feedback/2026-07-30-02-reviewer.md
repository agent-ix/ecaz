# Task 205 / 001-contract — the paper's actual contract values

Addendum to `2026-07-30-01-reviewer.md`. I read the paper directly
(`~/dev_bak/papers/distributedann-2509.06046.pdf`) and transcribed the two
algorithms verbatim into
`reviews/task-205/003-ab/artifacts/paper-algorithm-citations.md` so these
citations are checkable from inside the packet.

My seq-01 feedback asked for "`L` as a real parameter" and "`l` on the order of
`BW` or `L`." The paper is more specific than that, and in one place I was
imprecise. Corrections and exact values below — please write these into FR-079 /
FR-081 rather than my earlier paraphrase.

## 1. `H_C` is a heap of size `L`; `L ≥ max(BW, k)`

Algorithm 2's Input line reads, verbatim:

> Candidate size **L ≥ max(BW, k)**.
> Initialize result heap H_R of size k, **candidate heap H_C of size L**.

So `L` is a first-class search parameter with a stated lower bound, alongside
`BW`, `H`, `k`, and `k_head`. `t = peek_worst(H_C)` is then the worst of the `L`
retained candidates — a real floor, because the heap is bounded.

FR-081 should record `L` with the `L ≥ max(BW, k)` constraint, and the per-round
derivation as `t = peek_worst(H_C)` over that bounded heap. FR-079 should carry
`L` as the candidate limit (see next point).

## 2. `l` **is** `L` — a constant, not a budget

This is the correction that matters most. Algorithm 2 calls:

> Let {R_i}, {C_i} = **NodeScoring(K, t, L, q, q_SDC)**.

The candidate limit handed to Algorithm 1 is `L` itself, unchanged on every one
of the `H` rounds. It is not a function of the round index, not a function of
remaining hops, and not a separate tunable. My seq-01 phrasing ("on the order of
`BW` or `L`") was looser than the paper — the answer is exactly `L`, and `l`
should not exist in the spec as an independent knob. FR-079 should define the
owner-side limit as "the coordinator's candidate heap size `L`."

The current `remaining_rounds × BW` derivation has no counterpart in the paper.

## 3. The truncation is per response, not per node

Algorithm 1's last two lines sit **outside** the `for all n_i` loop:

> **for all** n_i **do** … **end for**
> Sort R and partial-sort C up to l
> Truncate C to l

The scoring service batch-reads every requested key, accumulates candidates
across all of them into one `C`, and truncates once. My seq-01 note on
`002-implementation` raised this as a P3; the paper makes it decisive rather than
stylistic. This is where the truncation power lives — see §5.

FR-079's owner contract should say the prune/sort/truncate applies to the merged
candidate set for the whole request, not per requested `vec_id`.

## 4. Threshold direction — ec_distann is safe, and stricter than the paper

Algorithm 1 keeps a candidate when `d_OPQ(q_SDC, p) < t`: a strict inequality on
a distance, so candidates *equal* to the threshold are dropped.

`prune_and_limit_neighbors` keeps `-code_dist >= threshold`, i.e. retains ties.
That is the conservative direction and it is what makes the equivalence argument
work, so **do not change it to match the paper**. But the contract should state
the divergence and why: ec_distann retains threshold ties deliberately, because
its equivalence claim is stronger than the paper's (the paper never claims
result-identity with an unpruned run).

## 5. Why `L=200` copied from the paper would still be inert here

Per round, before truncation, a batch produces up to `BW × R` candidates:

| | BW | R (degree) | candidates/round | `l` | effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| paper | 128 | 72 | 9,216 | L=200 | 46× cut |
| ec_distann today | 4 | 32 | 128 | ~350–396 | none possible |
| ec_distann with paper's L | 4 | 32 | 128 | 200 | **still none** |

At BW=4 and degree 32 a round cannot produce 200 candidates, so `l=200` never
binds. Copying the paper's constant would reproduce the current null result with
a more defensible-looking derivation, which is the worst outcome available here.

`L` has to be chosen for this regime. With `k=10` and `BW=4`, `L ≥ max(BW, k)`
allows anything from 10 up; something like `L=32` or `L=64` makes `t` a genuine
floor **and** gives `l` a 2–4× cut against 128 candidates/round. Please state the
chosen `L` and its justification in the contract, and treat "which `L`" as a
measured question rather than a copied constant.

Note the split this implies: at BW=4 the **threshold** is the working mechanism
and the **limit** is marginal; the paper's 46× truncation is a property of its
wide-beam regime. That is Task 206's territory, and it means Task 205 can
demonstrate `t` on its own but cannot demonstrate `l` at anything like the
paper's strength. Worth saying so in the task rather than discovering it in the
A/B.

## 6. The ~6× is not this feature's number

Task 205's Why section reads eq. (2) as quantifying the pushdown's saving.
Footnote 6 attributes the ~6× to transmitting scores instead of full nodes, and
then adds:

> We increase the savings further by pruning any neighbors that are worse than
> the current worst member of the candidate heap before returning to the
> orchestration service.

ec_distann already transmits scores rather than nodes, so the ~6× is **already
banked** in the current architecture. The threshold prune is an unquantified
increment on top of it. The contract should not carry a ~6× expectation for this
feature, and the A/B should not be judged against one.

over the `L`-bounded heap; the owner limit as `L` itself, applied once per
response over the merged candidate set; the deliberate tie-retention divergence;
