# Task 205 / 001-contract — REQUEST CHANGES

The equivalence argument is **correct**. I checked it and could not break it. The
problem is that it is correct for a reason the document does not state: the
conditions it imposes to guarantee equivalence are the same conditions that make
the feature do nothing. See `reviews/task-205/003-ab/feedback/2026-07-30-01-reviewer.md`
for the measurement — the pushdown pruned exactly zero neighbors across 150 scans.

## The argument checks out

Threshold direction: the beam stores `-ip`, `t = min over beam of (-dist) = ip of
the worst retained member`, and the owner keeps a neighbor when
`-code_dist >= t`, i.e. when its estimated ip is at least the worst retained
member's. Ties survive (`>=`, not `>`) and are ordered by
`(code_distance, vec_id)`, so the wire response is deterministic. Correct.

Soundness: if the beam holds at least `remaining_expansion_budget` candidates and
every one of them is at least as good as `t`, then a neighbor scoring below `t`
can never be selected in the remaining rounds, so it cannot be expanded, so it
cannot contribute a hit — hits come only from expanded nodes' `exact_dist`.
Tombstones are unaffected since they are dropped by `exact_dist = NULL` on the
coordinator side, not by the owner filter. Correct.

## P1 — the argument should state what the guarantee costs

The task asked for "an explicit statement of what breaks the equivalence." The
document has that (stale threshold, `l` too small). It is missing the other half,
which turns out to be the decisive half: **what makes the equivalence free.**

The guard is "send `t` only once the live beam already holds at least
`remaining rounds × BW` candidates." Since `beam` is an unbounded `BinaryHeap`
that accumulates every candidate ever enqueued, that condition is met early *and*
`t` is by then the score of the worst of ~890 accumulated candidates — a floor so
permissive nothing fails it. The safety condition and the vacuity are the same
fact viewed twice.

The paper's `t = peek_worst(H_C)` is the worst member of a heap **bounded at
`L`**. That bound is the mechanism; without it there is no threshold worth
sending. `L` does not appear in this contract, in FR-079/FR-081, or in the code.
Please add it — the contract slice is the right place for it — and re-derive the
equivalence claim against the `L`-bounded heap, where it becomes a real (and more
interesting) argument rather than a trivially satisfied one.

The same applies to `l`: `remaining rounds × BW` is not the paper's candidate
limit, it is an upper bound on total future consumption. At BW=4, H=100, degree
32, it is ~11× larger than the largest response that could ever be produced.

## P2 — the soundness guard counts entries that cannot be expanded

`beam.len()` includes candidates already present in `expanded`; the pop loop
skips them lazily rather than removing them at expansion time. So the guard's
"the beam holds at least `remaining_expansion_budget` candidates" can be satisfied
by entries that can never be expanded again, and the live count can be strictly
smaller than the guard believes.

This is harmless today only because `t` is vacuous. It becomes a genuine
recall-correctness hole the moment `t` is tightened to an `L`-bounded value —
which is exactly the change I am asking for above. Please either track a live
unexpanded count or filter before the length test, and say so in the contract.

## P3 — FR-079 framing

FR-079 frames `code_threshold` as an optimization that "may prune true results."
If the `L`-bounded derivation lands with the equivalence argument attached, that
framing should be amended rather than left standing alongside a contradictory
claim in this packet. The task text anticipated this ("If equivalence cannot be
established, the feature ships as opt-in and labeled") — the current state is the
inverse case, where equivalence holds but is unearned, and neither branch of the
task's instruction quite covers it. Worth stating plainly in the contract.

Request stays open.
