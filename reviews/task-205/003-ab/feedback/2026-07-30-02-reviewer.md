# Task 205 / 003-ab — paper grounding for the re-run and the NFR-021 verdict

Addendum to `2026-07-30-01-reviewer.md`, after reading
DistributedANN (arXiv:2509.06046) directly. Verbatim algorithm and

Both seq-01 P1s survive the reading, and both get sharper. One correction to my

## 1. NFR-021 — the paper does not contain this invariant

My seq-01 argued NFR-021's Statement contradicts its Measurement table. The paper
settles which side is right, and it is not the table.

DISTRIBUTEDANN **accepts** growth in per-machine state and scales the roster:

- §2.2 introduces ~10× space amplification deliberately (eq. 1, footnote 3),
  precisely because "for a sufficiently large index, the array of compressed
  vectors will not be able to fit in a single machine."
- Table 1 reports 780 TiB SSD and 42 TiB memory against 270 / 18 for clustered
  partitioning — it spends ~2.9× the storage and reports it as a cost worth
  paying.
- "DISTRIBUTEDANN is bound by SSD space and can continue scaling to over 100k QPS
  in the same footprint."
- The corpus is "spread across over a thousand machines."

What the paper actually bounds is one component: the **orchestration service**.
"Because this service has a small amount of persistent state, it can be hosted on
many machines with low overhead." That state is `H_R` (size `k`) and `H_C` (size
`L`) — heap-sized, independent of N. Everything O(N) lives in the key-value store
across the roster.

So NFR-021's **Statement** is a faithful restatement of the paper: sharded O(N)
is fine, coordinator-resident O(N) is not. Its **Measurement** row is not from the
paper at all. Nothing in DISTRIBUTEDANN implies per-node bytes stay flat as N
grows — that only holds if the roster grows with N, which is exactly the
assumption the `≤ 2.0` threshold encodes silently and which a fixed 3-node
fixture does not satisfy.

**Ask, unchanged but now grounded:** withdraw the NFR-021 inadmissibility verdict
from this packet, and escalate the Measurement row to NFR-021's owner citing
§2.2, §2.4 and Table 1. The discriminating measurement Task 204 now emits — the
coordinator's own `physical_benchmark_storage_node` row, `graph_side_bytes=0` on
owner-control versus `1,659,518,976` on the replica arm — is the paper-faithful
test and needs no cross-scale ratio.

One item that cuts the other way and should go in the escalation: NFR-021 permits
a coordinator-resident head only if capacity `C` is "a constant independent of
N." The paper's head is 2.5 billion vectors on a 50 billion vector index — ~5% of
N — and it handles that by making it "a conventional **sharded** in-memory ANN
index." The paper's head is proportional *and* sharded; ec_distann's is constant
*and* coordinator-resident. Both pass NFR-021 as written, by opposite routes.
That is worth resolving before Task 207 touches head reconstruction.

## 2. The re-run cannot be judged against ~6×

Task 205's Why section reads eq. (2) as the pushdown's expected saving. It is
not. Footnote 6 attributes ~6× to transmitting scores instead of full nodes, then
says "**We increase the savings further** by pruning any neighbors that are worse
than the current worst member of the candidate heap."

ec_distann already transmits scores, not nodes. The ~6× is **already banked** in
the current architecture — it is the difference between today's design and a
naive virtual-disk one, not between the control and candidate arms. The threshold
prune is an unquantified increment on top.

Please set the re-run's expectation accordingly. A 10–30% response-byte reduction
would be a real result here; anything near 6× would mean something else changed.

## 3. Correction to my seq-01 recommendation, and a regime problem

Seq-01 said to set `l` "on the order of `BW` or `L`." The paper is exact: the
limit passed to Algorithm 1 **is `L`**, the candidate heap size, constant across
all `H` rounds. My phrasing was looser than the source; use `L`.

More importantly, copying the paper's `L=200` would leave the feature inert here:

| | BW | degree R | candidates/round | `l` | truncation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| paper | 128 | 72 | 9,216 | L=200 | 46× |
| this A/B | 4 | 32 | 128 | ~350–396 | none |
| this A/B with L=200 | 4 | 32 | 128 | 200 | **none** |

At BW=4 and degree 32 a round cannot produce 200 candidates. A re-run with
`L=200` would reproduce the current null result behind a more defensible-looking
derivation — the worst available outcome, because it would read as "Algorithm 1
was implemented faithfully and does not help."

This splits the task's benchmark gate in a way worth stating up front:

- **The threshold `t` is measurable at BW=4.** With `L` bounded to something like
  32 or 64, `peek_worst(H_C)` is a genuine floor and should prune a measurable
  fraction of the 128 candidates/round. This is Task 205's real deliverable.
- **The limit `l` is not measurable at BW=4.** Its 46× cut is a property of the
  paper's wide-beam regime (BW=128, R=72). At BW=4 it is worth at most a 2–4×
  cut with an aggressive `L`, and zero with a paper-sized one.

Task 205's Non-goals forbid changing BW, deferring it to Task 206. That remains
right for attribution — but the packet should record that `l`'s effect is
*structurally* unobservable in this regime, so Task 206 inherits it rather than
Task 205 being marked as having tested it. Otherwise "Algorithm 1 measured, no
effect" enters the record for a component that was never in a position to show
one.

## 4. What the re-run should report

Keep the existing matrix, configs, corpus SHAs, and the baseline/control/candidate
shape — that method was sound. Add:

- `neighbors_pruned` and `pushdown_rounds_with_threshold` per arm (seq-01 P2,
  now blocking). A prune rate of zero must be visible as zero, not inferred from
  `traversal_frontier_insert` sample counts as I had to do.
- the chosen `L`, and a short sweep over it — `L` is now a search parameter and
  its recall/latency effect is not obvious a priori.
- response bytes and per-round transport wait, as before, but judged against a
  10–30% expectation rather than 6×.
- an explicit statement that `l`'s truncation is inert at BW=4 and why.

The `L`-bounded heap is itself a behavior change (it can evict candidates the
unbounded frontier retained), so it needs its own A/B cell against the current
unbounded beam, separate from the threshold. Two changes are landing together;
don't stack them, per the task's own attribution rule.
