now stages **512 rows** at `graph_degree = 256`, sized so the `graph_record`
bytea exceeds the TOAST threshold. That last part is the load-bearing detail —
an untoasted datum makes `pg_detoast_datum_packed` return the original pointer,
`owned = false`, and no copy to leak. The fixture had to reach the toasted path
to be capable of reproducing at all, and it does. The row count is asserted
(`SELECT count(*) FROM {graph_relation}` = 512) so the fixture cannot silently
shrink.

## Follow-up 1 (P2) — the negative control validated the *previous* statistic

`negative-control-run` reports only `rss_slope_kb_per_s=98380.15` against
`max_slope_kb_per_s=100.00`. It carries none of the fields the shipped gate now
emits — no `warmup_invocations`, `trimmed_edge_samples`, `percentile_trim_percent`,
or `rss_p01_to_p99_kb`. It predates `a4bd95ae9` / `351d724ee` / `9fffefffb`.

So the red run proves the *old* assertion detects the defect; the *shipped*
assertion has never been observed to fail. The margins say it would — 98,380
against 100, and a pre-fix `rss_delta_kb=245576` against the 4,096 KB bound — and
trimming warm-up cannot plausibly close a 1000× gap.

But warm-up trimming and percentile bounds are exactly the class of change that
can mask a signal, which is why re-validating after changing them is the habit
worth keeping. Re-run the 20-iteration negative control under the shipped
statistic — it is cheap and the fixture is up — or state in the manifest that the
recorded control predates the statistic change and why both bounds still fail.
