## Verdict

**ACCEPT the STOP decision.** No candidate is promotable and the packets are

The findings below are corrections to the *interpretation*, not to the data.

## F1 (P1) — the gateway arm was structurally unable to differ from the control

`control_gateway_jaccard=1.0` is read in packet 003 as "set cover ultimately
selected the same membership as control", i.e. as evidence that gateway
selection does not help. The builder cannot support that reading.

In `training_gateway_set_cover_nodes` → `analyze_gateway_split` →
`gateway_set_cover_nodes` (`head_sample.rs`):

- the set-cover candidate universe is only the nodes appearing in
  `ranking.per_query` — candidate width 32 over 200 training queries, so at most
  6,400 distinct nodes, drawn from the *same* `rank_training_candidates` output
  the frequency control ranks;
- the greedy loop `break`s at `gain == 0`, and the manifest records **127**
  positive-marginal picks; and
- the remaining **3,969 of 4,096 slots (96.9%)** are filled by
  `frequency_landmark_nodes(...)` — literally the control policy — via the
  `fallback` loop.

So ~97% of the persisted head is the control by construction, and the 3% the
objective controls is chosen from the control's own candidate pool. Jaccard 1.0
is close to a structural certainty rather than a measured refutation. Packet
002's advisory F2 frames the tail fill as a reason membership *might* differ
spuriously; the real hazard is the opposite — membership essentially could not
differ.

Consequence: the honest claim is "a marginal-coverage objective applied to ≤3%
of a 4,096 head, over a candidate pool the control already saturates, changes
nothing", not "gateway selection does not improve bounded recall". If the
family is ever retried, the objective must control the whole cap (score every
slot by marginal coverage) over a candidate pool substantially larger than the
cap. Please record that distinction in `plan/design/ec-distann-recall-latency-
roadmap.md` so `HEAD-17`–`HEAD-20`/`HEAD-33` are not retired on this evidence.

## F2 (P1) — the diversified "basin" is not the basin that was attributed

Phase-1 attribution defines a basin by real traversal: `run_gateway_traversal`
runs `distann_orchestrated_search` over the production `VamanaGraph` at
BW4/H100 and takes expanded-set Jaccard. The runtime selector that was actually
A/B'd, `DistannPhysicalHeadIndex::search_exact_basin_diverse`, defines a basin
as `head_basin()` — a 32-wide `greedy_search` over the **head** graph (4,096
nodes), never touching the base graph.

Two different objects share one name. The near-saturated 0.988 mean overlap the
packet reports is a property of 32-wide walks inside a 4,096-node head graph,
and driving it down by 0.0004 says nothing about redundancy in the traversal
that determines recall. The negative result is honest for what was measured;
the pre-registered intervention ("penalizes shared traversal basins",
`HEAD-13`–`HEAD-15`, `HEAD-32`) was not the thing measured. State this in the
handoff so 186/188 do not treat traversal-basin diversity as refuted.

## F3 (P2) — the 46–48 ms/query cost belongs to the prototype, not the idea

`search_exact_basin_diverse` truncates to `8 × seed_count = 256` candidates and
then calls `head_basin()` — a full head-graph greedy search — once per
candidate, followed by an O(k·n) penalized-rank scan with pairwise overlap
recomputed each round. 256 graph searches per query is the cost. Reporting
"basin diversification costs ~47 ms/query" as a property of diversity-aware seed
selection overstates a fixable prototype cost. It does not change the STOP
(recall was flat, so cost is moot), but the ledger should say "this prototype",
not "this family".
