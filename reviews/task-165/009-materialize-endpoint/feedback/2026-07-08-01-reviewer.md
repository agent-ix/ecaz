## Findings

### P2 - `resolve_owned_rows` slices raw node records without validating tag or length

After the directory lookup, `resolve_owned_rows` reads raw bytes and immediately slices out the heap TID and flags (`src/am/ec_distann/remote_endpoint.rs:285`, `src/am/ec_distann/remote_endpoint.rs:287`, `src/am/ec_distann/remote_endpoint.rs:291`). It does not verify the tuple tag, encoded length, or flag mask before indexing into the buffer. A corrupt directory target, wrong tuple type, or short tuple can therefore panic on slice bounds or silently decode non-node bytes as a heap TID instead of returning a classified `EC_INTERNAL` storage fault.

Other ec_distann paths already guard this. `read_node` rejects non-node records before decoding (`src/am/ec_distann/reader.rs:198`), `DistannNodeTuple::decode_into` checks exact length/tag/flags (`src/am/ec_distann/tuple.rs:172`), and tombstone scan checks tag and minimum length before slicing (`src/am/ec_distann/dml.rs:89`). Please reuse `DistannNodeTuple::decode` or add equivalent tag/length/flag validation in `resolve_owned_rows`, then add a narrow malformed-record regression if practical.

## What was done well

- Factoring epoch validation, ownership validation, and owned-absent handling into one `resolve_owned_rows` helper is the right way to keep the ctid and payload endpoints aligned.
- The endpoint is clear that owner-side ctid shipping is only a foundation and does not by itself solve remote heap materialization.
