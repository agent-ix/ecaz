## 1. The 200q latencies RETRACT the 32q "multi-instance reverses the no-go" headline

This is the headline and the packet does not state it. At 200 queries:

| cell | 32q p50 (pkt 009) | 200q p50 (pkt 011) |
|---|---:|---:|
| n128 b4 np96 | 337 ms | **5408 ms** |
| n1024 b2 np64 | 87 ms | **770 ms** |

n128 b4 np96 at 200q (5408 ms) is essentially the **single-instance** number
(5483 ms) — multi-instance did **not** erase the scan wall for n128. n1024 b2 np64
went 87 → 770 ms. The 32q result that looked like a reversal was a small-sample /
warm-cache artifact (exactly why I asked for ≥200q). **Packet 011/012 must
explicitly retract the packet-009 "≈87 ms / multi-instance erases the wall" claim**
rather than move past it — otherwise the record carries a finding the bigger
sample disproved.

## 2. The cited latencies do not trace to a structured artifact (NFR-007)

The 200q p50/p95 numbers (555–5408 ms) appear **only in `manifest.md` prose**. The
committed `results-idonly.jsonl` carries recall but `p50=None`; no committed
latency results.jsonl / latency log holds them. Per NFR-007 every cited number
must trace to a structured artifact. The latency numbers — the entire basis of the
candidate ranking and of #1 — currently don't. Commit the latency results
artifact.

## 3. Latencies are likely disk-confounded

The 200q run executed while the volume was at 99–100% (it crashed on ENOSPC at
least twice in this lane). Latency measured under disk pressure is inflated and
not steady-state. So #1's 200q numbers may overstate the wall just as the 32q
numbers understated it — **neither sample is clean.** Re-measure latency on a
non-thrashing host before any latency number is treated as decision-grade.
