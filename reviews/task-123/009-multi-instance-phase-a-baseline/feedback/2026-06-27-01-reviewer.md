# Review — Task 123 multi-instance Phase A baseline (packet 009)

**Verdict: major preliminary result — it vindicates the reopening and materially
reverses the single-instance no-go. Accept as a strong directional signal, NOT
yet as the new verdict: confirm at full query count with a same-substrate floor
and the per-stage taxonomy before any cost conclusion.**

## The headline (and it's a big one)

On the contained multi-instance lane (1 coordinator + 3 workers, same host,
network RTT ≈ 0), the multi-second scan wall from the single-instance runs is
**gone**:

- `n1024 b2/tr50/f8` reaches **recall 1.0000 at np64 with p50 87.3 ms / p95
  90.4 ms** — versus the single-instance `n128 b4 np96` 5483 ms for the same
  recall. That is the single-instance cost verdict overturned on the real
  distributed executor.
- `n1024 b2` also dominates `n128 b4`: faster to recall 1.0 (87 ms @ np64 vs
  337 ms @ np96) and a smaller coordinator index (246 vs 392 MiB).
- The production-read profile confirms this is the genuine distributed path
  (`result_source=remote_heap_candidates`, 2048-3072 remote pids, 93-96
  dispatches), and the dominant stage scales with selected remote pids — exactly
  the routing×fan-out coupling the amended task is about.

This is the result the reopening existed to find: the single-instance "scan path
is the wall / flat dominates" conclusion was substantially a degenerate-topology
artifact. Good — and the honesty about what this packet does and doesn't show is
exemplary (instrumentation gap, ENOSPC, non-additive profile buckets all called
out).

## Why this is not yet the verdict — three gaps to close

1. **32 queries is under-powered.** Every prior single-instance verdict used
   200 queries; this uses 32. Recall 1.0000 on 32 queries (320 truth) and these
   p50/p95s are an encouraging signal, not a decision-grade measurement —
   especially since the whole point is to *reverse* a prior verdict. Re-run the
   two cells at **≥200 queries** before the new cost picture is stated as fact.
   (HARD rule in this repo: confirm claims with facts at decision-grade; a
   verdict reversal needs at least the sample size of the verdict it overturns.)

2. **No same-substrate floor.** The 87 ms is compared, implicitly, against the
   *single-instance* flat floor (161-204 ms). That's apples-to-oranges. The
   robust, unimpeachable headline is **SPIRE multi-instance vs SPIRE
   single-instance** (5483 → 87 ms at recall 1.0) — state it that way. If you
   also want the flat comparison, measure flat exact on the same host/setup
   (and note a single-node flat scan has no distributed analog at this scale, so
   the comparison's meaning shifts — which is itself the point: at the scale/
   topology where SPIRE is meant to live, flat is not the comparator).

3. **The four-dimension taxonomy is still missing** (the core efficiency
   deliverable of the amended task). `payload_bytes_sum=0` because the suite
   projects only `id`; the profile buckets are aggregate percentiles, not an
   additive scoring/traversal/planning/communications split; no per-worker object
   bytes. Packet 010 starts closing this — but until the split exists you have
   "multi-instance is fast," not "here is which stage costs what and what to
   improve." That attribution is the deliverable.

## Recommendation

Carry `n1024 b2/tr50/f8` forward as the **leading multi-instance candidate** —
it's the first SPIRE config to reach recall 1.0 at competitive latency on the
real executor. Next packet: same two cells at ≥200 queries, with the per-stage
taxonomy from the 010 instrumentation and a realistic projection (so
communications bytes are real, not `id`-only). Do not flip the Task 123 status to
a positive verdict on the 32-query run; this is "reopened, strong early signal,"
not "viable, proven."
