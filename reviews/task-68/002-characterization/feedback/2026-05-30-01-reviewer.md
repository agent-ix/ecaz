## Measurement methodology — packet 001 issues that affect ranking

(See `reviews/task-68/001-spire-build-timing-notices/feedback/2026-05-30-01-reviewer.md`
for the full discussion.)

**Blocker 2 — Phase double-counting.** The current notice's
`draft_ms` includes `recursive_kmeans_ms + recursive_assignment_ms`,
and `object_store_ms` equals `top_graph_ms` when top-graph is
enabled. Today the inner timings are small (≤ 2 ms vs 19282 ms
draft, 252/252 for object_store/top_graph) so the ranking isn't
distorted in absolute terms, but **the 252 ms attributed to
"object-store/publish" is actually 100 % top-graph construction
with zero separate object-store work measured**. The "Top-graph /
object-store publish" entry in the ranked recommendations is
misleading; rewrite as either "Top-graph construction (252 ms)
plus separate object-store flush (unmeasured, expected small)" or
fix the notice and re-run.

**Blocker 3 — Recursive k-means call-count anomaly.** The
characterization reports `recursive_kmeans_calls=1` at
nlists=128, fanout=8. With recursive fanout 8 and target 128
leaves, the recursion should iterate at least one parent level to
collapse 128 children into 16 then into 2 (≈ 2 calls). Confirm
whether this is:

- A real property of the current termination logic (e.g. fanout
  override at the first parent level), in which case document it
  in the next packet's manifest. The hypothesis "k-means is tiny
  in the recursive path because we hit target_fanout in one pass"
  needs the call-graph audit before Phase 2 trusts it.
- An instrumentation gap (only the outermost call is wrapped). In
  which case `recursive_kmeans_ms=2 ms` is wrong-by-omission and
  the ranking under-reports recursive k-means cost.

Either way, the ranking's #4 "shared k-means training is no longer
the largest phase after Task 69" claim needs the audit to stand.
