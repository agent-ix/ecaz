## Finding

### P1 before enabling skips — the live call site violates the driver's exact-score contract

`drive_lazy_rerank` documents and proves safety assuming `fetch_exact(i)` returns
the candidate's exact heap-f32 score (`src/am/ec_ivf/lazy.rs:288-312`). The proof
keeps `worst_kept` as the largest exact score among reranked candidates and then
compares the suffix lower bound against that exact floor.

The current scan call site builds `approx_scores` from `candidate.score` and
passes `|i| approx_scores[i]` as the `fetch_exact` closure
(`src/am/ec_ivf/scan.rs:2239-2259`). The nearby comment explicitly says these
approximate frontier scores are fed as the driver's exact-score input so
`worst_kept` becomes finite (`scan.rs:2243-2254`).

That is safe today only because the production call also passes
`RaBitQLazyBound::default()` (`slack = +inf`) and `min_kept = rerank_len`, so no
skip can fire. But it is not safe to claim a finite bound or future k-cap can
activate skips "with no further changes to this call site" (`scan.rs:2231-2237`).
Once skips can fire, the driver would compare a lower bound on exact scores
against a floor derived from approximate scores, not exact heap-f32 scores. That
breaks the Task 112 contract and can be recall-unsafe whenever approximate and
exact order diverge.

Before Task 112 can claim the lazy win or close the heap-fetch-reduction goal,
the activation path needs one of these changes:

- move heap/source fetch + exact rescore into the driver closure so
  `worst_kept` is actually exact when the stop predicate is evaluated;
- or change the lazy driver contract and proof to a different safe quantity, with
  tests that cover approximate/exact disagreement under a finite bound and a
  lowered floor.

The current no-skip wiring can remain as instrumentation/foundation, but the
packet and task closeout should not describe the seam as ready to activate
without this contract fix.
